# Tower Delve

A playable, original mobile-first incremental tower RPG. Canvas draws the dungeon and pixel entities; accessible DOM controls provide navigation, statistics, inspection, and progression. No backend or runtime game engine.

## Run

Use Node.js 22.18+ (tested with 24.15) and npm:

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
npm run preview
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. In the forest before a run, tap an open tile to walk to it, fighting monsters and collecting items along the route. Swipes move one tile in any cardinal direction. Keyboard arrows / WASD also work; on-screen arrows are off by default and can be enabled in Settings. Monsters on manual routes are fought even when lethal. Missing keys stop movement at the necessary door, with a fading red X; unreachable wall targets get the same feedback at the tapped spot. Routing prefers an available detour over a door for which no key is held. A first tap on a tile highlights it and shows what's there (a monster's stats, an item, a door's keys); once **Pathfinder** (1 Courage, after Movement Speed in the Courage tree, left of Focus) is owned, it also draws the route there on the board and adds a Route totals box of what happens along it (the HP lost, the keys spent, what's picked up). A second tap walks there either way.

Inside a Tower or Delve run, your **hand** of cards moves the hero instead; taps only inspect, and swipes and keys do nothing (except in Dev mode, which also starts the hand paused). A new profile's hand is its whole deck, in priority order: **Stairs** (the stairs up; in the Delve, the highest open tile in view, which its card text mentions only once Into the depths is owned), **Door** (a door you hold the keys for), **Yellow Key** (a yellow key, never a blue or red one), and **Monster** (any monster that can be fought, even a lethal one). Five more cards join the deck from the Inspiration tree: **Heal** (a potion; the Heal skill, 3 Inspiration, below Regen), **ATK Up** (an ATK pickup; the ATK Up skill, 5 Inspiration, under Gear), **DEF Up** (a DEF pickup; the DEF Up skill, 5 Inspiration, under ATK Up) and **Blue Key** (a blue key; the Blue Key skill, 2 Inspiration, after the Archives, below DEF Up), and **Key Siphon** (the Key Siphon skill, 5 Inspiration, below Into the depths, the start of the tree's middle path), which moves nowhere: when its turn comes it trades Max HP training levels, for the rest of the run only, for a yellow key, the hero losing the max HP those levels give and standing still for the turn (undo takes it back). The first use in a run takes 1 level, the second 2, the third 3, and so on, and its card's tooltip in the run says how many times it has been used. Its levels are the hero's own Max HP ranks and any bought with Silver that run; without enough left for its next use it greys out in the hand and is skipped, so where it sits in the hand decides when it fires. Below Buy Quantity, the middle path adds fifteen more (10 Inspiration each unless said): **Yellow Door** (5; a single yellow door, while you hold a yellow key; a Steel Door is DOOR's), **Heart Door** (5, left of Heart Door Resilience; a Heart Door), **Weak Enemy**, with **Base Enemy** (neither weak, strong, elite nor a boss) then **Strong Enemy** to its left and **Elite Enemy** then **Boss Enemy** (a boss or a Greater Boss) to its right, each heading for the closest enemy of its strength that can be fought, **Chest** (a closed treasure chest or area reward chest), **BK Siphon** and **RK Siphon** (Key Siphon's twins: DEF or ATK training levels for a blue or red key, each counting its own uses), **BK Trader** (3 yellow keys for a blue key, while 3 are held), **YK to HP** (a yellow key for 10% of max HP, only while that much is missing), **Red Key** (a red key), **Torch** (the closest lit torch, putting it out) and **Steel Door** (a Steel Door, which any one key opens, while you hold one). Two skills on that path open research instead: **Heart Door Resilience** (after Yellow Door, with Heart Door to its left) opens research making every Heart Door drain 5% less of your HP a level, for ten levels, and **Floor Skip Reward** (below BK Siphon, between Torch and Steel Door) opens research that, when the Skip badge on STAIRS climbs past a Tower floor, pays 10% a level (up to 110%) of the Gold that floor's battles and chests held; both are priced and timed like Focus Count, with one and two more levels. The siphons and trades move nowhere, like Key Siphon, and grey out while they can't act. Each new card rises from the bottom of the screen to its middle, green rays turning behind it and what it does written below, until you press it. Whenever the hero has no path to follow, the first card that can reach a target over open floor and items picks its closest one, and the hero walks the shortest path there, one step per tick of the movement speed (2 steps a second for a new hero); of equally short paths it takes the one stepping on the fewest items (keys, potions, ATK and DEF shards, treasure chests) and lit torches, so it walks round them unless they lie on the only shortest way, leaving them to the cards that aim at them (an area reward chest is no item here). Tapped routes do the same; the card glows as it moves, and keeps glowing while the hand is paused; playing on carries on along the same path. When no card can act, the hand pauses and End Run lights up: the run ends only when you end it. Whatever you do next (using an item, and later a skill) checks the hand again, and it plays on by itself once a card can act. The play/pause button (where Automove was, showing ▶ while paused and ❚❚ while playing, with the hero's movement speed under it, *3x* for 3 steps a second) pauses the hand to look around or use an item, and undo pauses it too. The ‹ and › arrows either side of the speed (a press anywhere on its side of the row counts) slow and speed the hero a step a second at a time, from 0 to 3 (more with research; › closes at the most allowed): slowing to 0 pauses the hand, and playing it again starts at 1. A new hero walks at 2; on the second floor the game pauses once, a pointing hand under ›, until the player speeds up to 3, then plays on. The forest's Automove keeps the speed last set in a run, but never walks slower than 3. The cards sit in the row under the board. Each run keeps the hand as it was ordered when the run went inside, however the hand is rearranged before the next.

Each Inspiration or Courage skill that can be bought wears its next rank's price in a circle on its corner, filled when the currency is there and outlined when not, so a path's cost shows at a glance. A **?** beside the Inspiration and Courage trees' headings explains how a run earns that currency (and, once you have climbed past floor 100 or 1,000, the slower rates beyond them). The Inspiration tree's first skill, **Combat Stance** (1 Inspiration), adds a **Deck** tab after Tower and Delve. The Deck page shows the hand's slots along the top: four, five with **Larger Hand** (1 Inspiration, after Training), and one more for each slot bought with Gems after that (50, 200, 400, 600, 800 and 1,000 Gems, up to eleven), the next for sale standing after them; dragging a card to another slot (or moving a focused card with the arrow keys) shifts each card in between over one, and the new order is saved at once. Below the hand sits the deck, locked until **Buildout** (1 Inspiration, after Combat Stance): then the deck's cards are laid out on a grid, those in the hand greyed out with a check that returns them to the deck, and pressing any other card adds it to the hand's first empty slot. Dragging one onto a hand slot puts it there instead: with room in the hand, the cards from that slot on slide one slot later; with the hand full, the card in that slot goes back to the deck (never STAIRS). A hand card shows an X in its corner while hovered (always, on touch screens) that also returns it to the deck, as does dragging it onto the deck (which lights up while it is held there). A hand must always hold STAIRS, which has no X and can only be reordered; every other card is optional. Every change is saved at once. While a Deck lesson is waiting, its tab carries a red dot. The Upgrades tab carries one too, once the first run that earns Inspiration is over, until you open it; and whenever a run that earned Inspiration ends, the Upgrades tab and the Inspiration tab on that page carry one in the forest while you hold enough Inspiration to buy a skill there, until you look at the Inspiration tree (likewise a Delve run that earned Courage, once the Courage tree is open, for the Courage tab); and so does the Gear tab once the Gear skill is bought, until you open it. The first visit teaches the drag: a note explains the order and a hand points at STAIRS, the other cards and tabs stay disabled until STAIRS is moved, and a dialog then praises the new order. With Buildout, the next lesson shows the X on MONSTER with a hand pointing at it, and again nothing else can be used until it is pressed; a dialog praises that (closed by pressing anywhere on it), and a note over the deck, dismissed by pressing it, explains adding cards.

**Gear** (1 Inspiration, after Buildout, left of it) adds the **Gear** tab, which opens on **Provisions**: Gold buys a Traveler's elixir (+20 max HP), an Aegis charm (+1 DEF) or a Whetstone (+1 ATK), each kept for good and added to every run from then on, a run already inside included. The tab shows your Gold in its own column beside its explanation; each provision says how many you own and, when one gives more than 1 of a stat (the elixir), the total they add; and a provision's icon glows when you buy one. Each one bought costs more than the last, on the same kind of schedule as Training bought with Silver inside a run (the elixir from 5 Gold, the charm from 10, the whetstone from 15), and that price compounds 5% more for each one already bought (the elixir: 5, 8, 12, 17, 24 … 111 for the 10th, 746 for the 20th, about 21,000 for the 50th). Once **Extra Key** (1 Courage, after Movement Speed in the Courage tree) is owned, Provisions also sell a **Yellow Key**, a yellow key carried into every run: the first costs 100 Gold and each after four times the last (400, 1,600, 6,400, 25,600 …). The Crafting tab stays closed for now.

**Equipment** opens by claiming Tower I's floor 60 checkpoint on the Goals screen, whose dialog offers to visit the Blacksmith. A **Blacksmith** then stands in the forest, up and left of the path; tapping it (or the Gear tab, which opens with Equipment even without the Gear skill) opens the Equipment screen. The hero wears one piece in each of nine categories (Weapon, Chestplate, Helmet, Gloves, Boots, Cape, Belt, Ring, Amulet); the inventory (up to 500 pieces) is shared, and the Tower's and the Delve's heroes each wear their own loadout. Pieces come in Common, Uncommon and Rare, shown by word, letter and colour. Every boss, on any floor, may then drop a Standard piece; the three Unique pieces of each category come only from Gem pulls (20 Gems, or 200 for ten; 72% Common, 25% Uncommon, 3% Rare, and the 100th pull in a row without a Rare in a category is a Rare). Every enemy drops upgrade materials, one per category, which level a piece with Gold up to its rarity's cap (20, 40, 60). Three copies of a piece at one rarity merge into the next, keeping the level of the one you choose; dismantling breaks unwanted pieces into their material, never returning what leveling cost. Locked and worn pieces are never dismantled or merged away. Besides its fixed lines, each piece has **effect slots**: one at Common, a second from level 21 (so Uncommon and above) and a third from level 41 (Rare), each holding an effect from its category's pool, never rarer than the piece. A new slot's first roll is free (choose one of three); after that, **Refine** pays for three new candidates beside the current effect, which stays unless you take one, and every Refine counts toward that slot's Refinement: every fifth guarantees a top-rarity candidate and every tenth earns a Choice of any effect outright. Merging keeps a piece's effects and Refinement. See [docs/EQUIPMENT.md](docs/EQUIPMENT.md).

**Focus** (5 Courage, after Movement Speed in the Courage tree, below it) is the player's own say in where the hero goes inside a run. Pressing a card in the hand under the board puts it ahead of the others: it glows white, the hand plays on (even if it was paused), and the card leads until the hero reaches its target, or until it has no path to one, when the hand's order leads again. Each run starts with 1 Focus, shown by a lightning bolt beside the potions in the stats, and each Focus that takes spends one; a Focus Count level completed during a run adds its use at once. With none left, the bolt flashes red; a card with no path to a target flashes red and costs nothing; the card already moving the hero can't be focused. Undo brings back the Focus uses and focused card of the moment they return to.

**Badges** (2 Courage, after Focus) opens a **Badges** box on the Deck page, beside the deck: the two share the space below the hand in two columns, each scrolling on its own when its cards or tokens don't fit. Above the box, **Draw ×1** (20 Gems) and **Draw ×10** (200 Gems) draw card badges: rounded tokens drawn by rarity (common 70%, rare 27%, epic 3%, among the rarities that still have one to draw), each continuing the same saved random sequence, so reloading never draws again. The first copy of a badge rises onto the screen with rays behind it; a repeat drops a coin onto its token, its copies bouncing up to their new count before the slash and the copies its next level takes appear, and a level reached sparkles onto the star in its corner. Ten drawn at once show one at a time, counting 1 / 10 to 10 / 10 (Skip jumps ahead), then all ten together, marking each one new or levelled up. The first copy opens level 1 and counts toward level 2; 3, 5, 8, 12, 20 and 32 copies reach levels 2 to 7 (80 in all), each token showing its copies toward the next under it, and a badge at level 7 is no longer drawn. Drag a token onto a card, in the hand or the deck, to attach it: a card holds one badge and a badge sits on one card, so dropping one on a card that holds another swaps them (or takes the other off, for a token from the box). An attached token keeps its place in the box with an X that takes it off, and its card wears a small mark of it at its top left. Pressing a token shows what it does at its level, its copies, and, for a gate, a slider to choose its threshold among those its level has opened; under the description, as when one is drawn, a line gives its value at every level, the one in use bright. Tokens move only in the forest; a run keeps the badges it went in with. A card **activates** when it reaches its target (or, like Key Siphon, acts in place): **HP** heals 1, 4, 9 … 49 HP, **HP %** 1–7% of max HP, **Silver Touch** pays 1–7 Silver (Silver bonuses apply), **Gold Touch** 1–7 Gold, and **Goldback** the same on the hand's last card only (Gold bonuses and the tower's apply; each target pays its Gold once, so undo can't repeat it), and **XP** pays 1, 4, 9 … 49 XP, rising with the floor as a kill's XP does. Gates keep their card from acting while their condition fails: **HP <** (HP below 100, 90, 75, 50, 35, 20 or 5% of max HP), **YK <**, **BK <** and **RK <** (holding fewer than 10, 8, 6, 4, 3, 2 or 1 yellow, blue or red keys); a Focus overrides a gate. **Stairward** has its card pick the target with the shortest walk on to the stairs (in the Delve, the highest), and **Skip Open Nodes** has it pass over any door or monster that opens no new ground, marking each with a violet X for the rest of the floor; each works on one floor in every 7 (level 1) to every floor (level 7). **Charge** lets its card's path go through up to 1–7 monsters (lethal ones too, as the hand always may) and doors whose keys the hero holds, fighting and opening them on the way to its target.

The Upgrades page shows a tab only once what unlocks it is owned: Inspiration from the start, then Training, Courage (with Into the depths), the Archives, and Legacy, Wisdom and Renown (Dev mode only for now). What opens Wayfinding, the Delve Automove skills, is still to be decided, so for now only Dev mode shows it; Dev mode shows them all.

**Archives** (5 Inspiration, after Larger Hand; Into the depths, 3 Inspiration, follows it) adds an Archives tab to the Upgrades page, after Courage: research that lasts between runs, paid in Gold and real time. An archivist works on one project at a time, a level at a time: pay the level's Gold, and it completes on the clock, whether the game is open, on another page, or closed. You start with one archivist and can hire up to four more for Gems (200, 500, 900 and 1400). Stopping research gives its Gold back and keeps the time already spent for when you start it again (paying again). **Rush**, beside Stop, completes the research at once for Gems: 1 Gem for each 10 minutes left, and 1 more for any part of 10 minutes. With **Auto-continue** ticked, an archivist starts the project's next level as soon as one completes, if you have the Gold; otherwise it waits idle. Each level completed pops up at the top of the screen, over everything, as "Research Complete!" with the project and level, for four seconds before it slides up out of sight; levels that complete together (such as while the game was closed) follow one another in order. The library lists only research whose requirements are met (Dev mode lists it all), and can be searched and filtered by category and status, and its History button opens a list of the levels completed lately. Each project's box is kept short: its name, categories and level on one line, its description, then what its next level changes, how long it takes and its Research button (showing its Gold) on one line. The first project, **Focus Count** (after the Focus skill), adds one Focus use to every run for each of its nine levels: level 1 takes 8 hours and costs 500 Gold, each level after takes 8 hours more, and costs 500 Gold times the level before it more (500, 1000, 2000, 3500, … 18,500). Below the Archives in the Inspiration tree, which grows taller than the screen and scrolls (drag it, or turn the mouse wheel; Ctrl and the wheel, or a pinch, zooms), **Greater Heal** (3 Inspiration, after Into the depths) opens the Archives' **Potion HP** research,, **Recovery** (10 Inspiration, after Regen Research) brings **percent potions** onto the floors (2% of potions), **Find Potion** (10 Inspiration, below Recovery) opens **Find Potion** training, which makes more of them percent potions, and **Shroud** (10 Inspiration, after Regen Research, beside Recovery) wraps the hero in a **shroud** that blocks the first 1 damage of every fight and opens **Shroud** training, and **Revive** (10 Inspiration, below Shroud) may raise a felled hero at full HP (see below). **Pocket Money** (1 Inspiration, after Into the depths, beside Greater Heal) opens the Archives' **Pocket Money** research: every run goes inside with 5 more Silver in hand a level, for 100 levels, priced and timed like Faster Trainers. **Spare Change** (3 Inspiration, below Pocket Money) makes every floor climbed for the first time in a run pay Gold: in the Tower each staircase taken up for the first time that run, in the Delve each new ten depth. It pays 3 Gold, and 1 more for each rank of the **Gold / Floor** training it opens (Utility, 1 training point a rank, or Silver inside a run), times the tower's Gold bonus; undo never makes a floor pay twice. It also opens the Archives' **Gold / Floor** research (listed last), +5% of that Gold a level for 100 levels, priced and timed like Potion HP. Below it, three more skills (2 Inspiration each) each open a Utility training row (1 point a rank, or Silver inside a run) and Archives research of the same name (100 levels, priced and timed like Gold / Floor): **Wealthy** opens **Silver Bonus**, which multiplies all Silver a run finds (×1, +1% a rank, and +3% a research level, the two multiplied: 30% of each makes ×1.69); **Loot** opens **Gold / Kill**, which multiplies each kill's Gold the same way but +3% a rank, before the tower's Gold bonus; **Wishing Well** opens **Silver / Floor**: every floor climbed for the first time in a run (above the run's highest, in the Tower) pays 3 Silver, and 3 more a rank, +5% a research level, then Silver Bonus. Silver keeps its fractions, like Gold, and the purse shows it rounded down. The shroud is whole again at the start of each fight and takes the enemy's strikes until it is spent; only then does HP fall. What it blocks rises off the hero in silver, beside the red of any damage that got through, the HUD shows it beside DEF once owned, and every damage forecast (the inspect box, route totals, the hand and Automove) counts it. Without Recovery every potion is a regular one; with it, the red, striped potions the Tower's floors roll (half of them) and half of the Delve's corridor-guard potions become percent potions, from the next run on: each restores the regular 35 HP plus 1% of max HP, and 0.25% more for each rank of **Potion %** training. **Regen** research (after **Regen Research**, 2 Inspiration, below Greater Heal, above Recovery and Shroud, which follow it; listed after Potion HP) makes every step regain 3% more of what Regen training gives a level, for 100 levels, priced and timed exactly like Potion HP, and counting at once. **Buy Quantity** (5 Inspiration, below Key Siphon) adds a quantity dropdown to Training, at the right of the trainers' row on the Training tab and of the group buttons under the hand inside a run: one press of a training-points button, or of a run card's Silver price, buys that many ranks at once (fewer only at a row's most), showing their total price, and the Training tab shows the value they reach. It offers x1 at first; the Archives' **Buy Quantity** research opens x5, x10, x100 and Max, a level each (1,000, 5,000, 25,000 and 100,000 Gold; 4, 12, 24 and 48 hours). Max buys as many as the points or Silver held pay for, its count ("x12") shown on the button beside their price. Trainers still train one rank at a time. **Potion HP** (listed first in the Archives) makes every potion restore 3% more HP a level, for 100 levels (up to four times as much), the Cinder Tonic included but not the red potion; each level counts the moment it completes, even mid-run. The first four levels are quick (15 seconds for 10 Gold, 1 minute for 25, 5 minutes for 50, 10 minutes for 75); then the formula starts over, so the m-th level after those takes m/4 hours and costs 100 × m Gold (15 minutes and 100 Gold at level 5, up to 24 hours and 9,600 Gold at level 100).

Fights loosen the classic Magic Tower rule that DEF equal to a monster's ATK makes it harmless: after every round of a fight the monster's ATK rises by 1% (rounded down), and by at least 1. High DEF still holds a monster off for a while, but never forever, and a fight with a monster the hero can barely scratch grows lethal instead of dragging on. The inspect panel's damage already counts the rise.

Stepping into a monster plays the fight out: the hero waits on its own tile and strikes first, the monster strikes back, and each strike's damage rises in red off whoever was struck (a darker red off the hero) while HP counts down in the stats. From the hero's first strike, the monster's HP shows in a red bar above it, draining with each strike and fading once the monster falls. **Instant Combat** (1 Courage, left of Movement Speed in the Courage tree) adds **Animate fights** to Settings (hidden until then, and on): turned off, each fight counts at once, in one step, and shows as a summary. A monster that falls raises nothing (it plainly lost all its HP), and only the damage the hero took rises off the hero. A fight the hero loses raises the damage each way at once, and the monster's HP bar stays, showing what it has left, so you can judge whether to undo. A revival shows the fight up to it the same way, and once its golden fire is out the rest of the fight follows as another summary. The HP the fight has cost so far shows in purple on the HP bar, just past the HP left, and shrinks away over a second once the fight is over. A health potion, picked up or crafted and drunk, does the opposite: the HP it heals shows in light red past the HP you had, the red bar fills up over it in a second, and the HP actually healed rises in green over the hero. The first round's strikes take 0.25 s each, and each later round runs at 90% of the one before, down to 0.05 s a strike. Once the monster falls the hero steps onto its tile. Either way, every reward picked up (an item, a chest's Gold and materials, a monster's drops) rises from its tile as its sprite for about a second, starting at once over anything still rising (what comes together, like a chest's Gold and materials, one after another); rewards with no sprite rise as "+N" text. A door raises each key it took with a minus sign, and a Heart Door, which needs no key but drains the hero's HP to 1, a heart with the HP it took. Taking the stairs snaps the hero straight onto the new floor with a brief white glow behind it, and while the hero stands on the board's bottom row the status line over it turns half see-through. Undo during a fight takes back the whole fight. A monster's inspect panel, and the messages about its fight, put its strength before its name (Weak, Strong, Elite or Boss), unless it is a normal one. Every monster beaten pays Gold by its strength: none for a weak one, 1 for a normal one, 2 for a strong one, 4 for an elite one and 5 for a boss. A boss, glowing red and gold with three chevrons and twice a strong monster's HP and ATK, guards the way up every ten floors: it stands in front of the stairs on Tower floors 10, 20, 30 and so on, and in the shaft below each Delve milestone gate, so it must be beaten to climb on. A Tower floor also hides a secret: put out every torch on it and a **Greater Boss** appears in a puff of smoke on the open floor nearest the stairs (once a floor each run). Ringed in violet and gold with four chevrons, it has twice a boss's HP, ATK and DEF and pays twice a boss's Gold, Silver and XP. On other Tower floors the stairs may stand open, behind a monster, or behind a yellow door. In Tower I and Delve I, blue keys and blue doors first appear on floor 21 (the Delve's equivalent floor 21, depth 200), the first floor past the floor 20 boss, and red ones on floor 51. In Tower I a blue or red door never stands alone on the way to the stairs: one there always stands in a fork beside another lane that needs neither colour (a yellow door, a monster), so a hero without the key can still climb, while optional rooms may lock treasure behind them; Tower II, Delve II and the rest have them from their first floors, since a hero who reaches them has met them already. The first floor's stairs can always be reached without a fight, so the starting hand's Stairs card climbs straight to floor 2 (and its Inspiration); floors 2 to 5 always have a yellow door in front of the stairs, a yellow key lying free near the entrance, and a key behind every door, so the starting hand can open its way up. On Tower I's first ten floors, about a third of the doorways on the way to the stairs hold a health potion where a monster would have stood. No monster on floors 1 to 10 can beat a new hero (100 HP, 12 ATK) in a single fight, but the floor 10 boss can. Strong monsters appear only from floor 11 and elites (from the next zone) only from floor 41, and Heart Doors only past floor 100 in the first tower (from floor 31 in later ones); the Delve counts its equivalent floors the same way. The stats show the Gold found so far this run beside ATK and DEF; undo never takes Gold back, so it never lowers the count.

Every monster beaten also pays **Silver**, a currency that lasts only for the run, spent on training for that run. A weak monster pays 1 Silver on floors 1–10, 2 on floors 11–20, and 1 more every ten floors after (the Delve counts every ten depth as a floor); a normal monster pays twice that, a strong one three times, an elite four times, and a boss five times. The stats show it beside the Gold. Undo takes back the Silver of the kills it undoes, and a new run starts with none.

**Training for the run.** Once **On the Job** is owned (1 Inspiration, after Buildout, right of it), a bar under the hand holds a wide button for each Training group: Offense, Defense and Utility (greyed until something in it is unlocked). Pressing one shows that group's Training rows in the hand's place, a card each, scrolled sideways: its name, where it stands now (the hero's stat, the percentage, or the Gold a new floor pays), and a button with the Silver price of the next level. Pressing the button pays the Silver and raises it at once, for the rest of this run only (more max HP comes with the HP to fill it); while the Silver isn't there the button's outline and face fade, its price still clear, and the card stays readable (it greys out only at its most). Pressing a card, or hovering over it, shows its description and its level of the most it can reach. The cards are exactly as tall as the hand's, so nothing else on the screen moves as they swap. Pressing the group's button again brings the hand back. A run starts at the hero's own Training level, so more Training between runs lets a run reach higher for the same Silver: the first level bought costs 3 Silver for max HP, 5 for ATK, DEF and Regen, 10 for Shroud, Potion % and the currency rows (Gold / Floor, Silver / Floor, Silver Bonus, Gold / Kill), and 20 for Find Potion and Revive. Each level after costs more than the last by a step plus the levels already bought, the step growing every five levels: +1, +3, +5 … for max HP (3, 5, 8, 12, 17, 23, 32 …), +1, +4, +7 … for the 5-Silver rows (ATK: 5, 7, 10, 14, 19, 25, 35 … 85 for the 11th), +2, +5, +8 … for the 10-Silver rows, and +4, +8, +12 … for the 20-Silver rows. A row goes no higher than its most on the Training tab (72 for Find Potion, 99 for Revive), or level 1,000. Each purchase is a turn of its own: undo takes it back, Silver and all. A level can be bought while a fight plays out too (not one shown as a summary): it counts from the fight's next strike, so ATK bought makes the hero's next strikes harder, DEF softens the monster's next strikes, and max HP raises the HP left at once, which can turn a losing fight around. The first run after On the Job is bought teaches the bar: a note over the hand explains spending Silver on upgrades for the run, and a pointing hand bounces over a group's button until the player opens a group and presses it again to close it.

Every monster defeated pays XP, more for stronger monsters and higher floors, and XP carries over between runs. Each level needs more XP than the last (with the cube of the level), faster than the XP a kill rises with height, so levels come more slowly the higher you climb, even replaying the floors below. The level shows as a large number at the front of the XP bar under the hero's portrait, which fills with the current level's progress; hovering it shows the XP gained toward the next level and what that level needs. Reaching a new level bursts fire outward from the hero, surrounds the hero with a glow for two seconds (fading over the last half second), and flashes "LEVEL UP!" over the board; once it fades, "+2" and the training points' golden arrow rise off the hero. Each level earns 2 **training points**, spent on the Upgrades page's Training tab, open from the start (each row names its level, *Max HP - Lv 5*, and *Lv 5 → 6* while a trainer trains it; the Upgrades and Training tabs wear a dot while there are points to spend): a table in three groups, Offense (ATK), Defense (max HP, DEF, then Regen, Shroud, Potion %, Find Potion and Revive once the Regen, Shroud, Recovery, Find Potion and Revive skills are owned; Regen gives HP back with every step taken in a run, 0.1 a rank at first and growing with the hero's level, a Rush turn counting as one step) and Utility (Gold / Floor, Silver / Floor, Silver Bonus and Gold / Kill once their skills are owned), each row with the training time its next rank still needs (less its time credit; counting down while a trainer trains it), its current value, the value one more rank makes it, and two ways to buy the next rank: training points (the arrow button) buy it at once, 1 point for a rank of any row; or, once the **Trainers** skill (1 Inspiration, after Buildout, below it) is owned, Gold (the coin button) pays a **trainer** to train it, which takes time but no points, at 20 Gold for each point the rank would cost times the trainer's rank number (20 for the first rank a trainer trains in that stat, 200 for the tenth). The two keep separate schedules: ranks bought with points never make a trainer's next rank dearer or slower. Until Trainers is owned the tab shows no trainers, timers, boost or auto-continue boxes. Buying a rank with points while a trainer trains it stops the trainer: the rank counts at once, the trainer's Gold comes back and the time it spent becomes time credit, as stopping it by hand does; the Gold and the time rise over the Gold held and the row's time to its next rank. Potion % adds 0.25% of max HP to what a percent potion restores, and Find Potion 0.25% to the chance that a potion is a percent potion (from 2% up to 20%, at 72 ranks), both the same at every level. A rank grows as the hero levels up, every rank already bought included: at level L a rank of max HP is worth 10 + L, of ATK 1 + L/5, of DEF 1 + L/12 and of Shroud 1 + L/10, fractions and all. A new hero starts with 100 max HP, no DEF and no shroud. Training applies at once to a run still in the forest. A stat can be reset for 2 Gems (its row's reset button), returning every point and all the Gold spent on it, and all the training time spent on it (its trainers' ranks, a rank in training, and its own time credit) into the **time bank**, shown beside a clock by the training points while there is any: any stat's next ranks trainers train start that far along, after that stat's own **time credit** (the time a stopped or rushed rank gave back to it), until it is used up. Both are taken off the time each row shows. A trainer's rank takes time, on the clock like the Archives' research: a stat's first four ranks take 15 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes and 10 minutes, and each after a quarter hour more than the last (15 minutes, 30, 45 …, 24 hours by the 100th), less with Faster Trainers research. One stat trains at a time, with one more for each trainer bought with Gems beside the trainer count (100, 300, 700, 1,200 and 1,800 Gems, up to six at once). A rank in training shows its countdown (press it to stop, get the Gold back and keep the time spent as credit) and a Gem button that finishes it at once: 1 Gem for each 10 minutes left, and 1 more for any part of 10 minutes. A stat a trainer is training moves up under the trainer count, in the order they started, and goes back to its group once its training ends. While it trains, a box with a looping arrow before its name sets it to **auto-continue**: while ticked, each rank its trainer finishes starts the next at once, as long as the Gold is there, ranks finished while the game was closed included. Each rank a trainer finishes, whatever page shows or while the game was closed, announces "Training Complete!" at the top of the screen and counts at once, a run inside included. The box at the top of the tab is the **training boost**: "×2" with its time left, or "Inactive", and an ad button, "+1h" (no ad plays yet: it is granted on the press), each press banking an hour more, up to four. While it lasts, ranks in training go twice as fast, their countdowns dropping two seconds a second, on the clock like the rest. **Faster Trainers** (2 Inspiration, after the Archives, right of Into the depths) opens Faster Trainers research: +2% training speed a level for 100 levels (+200%, a rank then taking a third of its time), the n-th level costing 250 × n Gold and taking 1.75 × n hours, about a year in all.

Movement transitions can be set to **Smooth** (default), **Fast**, or **Off (instant)** in Settings. This controls camera and player interpolation on both axes; Reduce motion overrides it with instant movement.

Matching open left/right world edges wrap to each other. Locked doors and enemies at the destination still apply. Openings have no solid frame across them and show continuation chevrons. Viewport cropping is not a wrap boundary; the world is 30 tiles wide. Vertical movement remains continuous upward.

**Undo** restores one move (a tapped route counts as a single move), including combat, pickups, gear, keys, doors, and the XP a kill paid (with any level it reached), but only on the floor you stand on: climbing or going down a Tower floor forgets the history, as does crossing one of the Delve's floor lines (each ten depth, either way) or a milestone gate. It cancels any queued route. A new profile has no undo, and the button stays hidden until **Rehearsed steps** (5 Inspiration, after the Archives) gives the first history slot (or Echoes of time and a level of Undo Count do); each level of **Undo Count** research in the Archives adds one: Rehearsed steps opens four levels of it and **Echoes of time** (Courage) four more, either opening it first, up to 9 moves. Each counts the moment it's bought or completed. History persists across refreshes.

Every way a run ends goes through one dialog, which says what brought it up and shows the same totals of what the run obtained: the floor reached, the run's highest floor, and the Gold, the Inspiration (or Courage) and the XP earned in this run, the first two with their icons, not the amounts held (undo takes a kill's XP back from it, as from the hero). Pressing End Run asks whether to end the run (or, when no card in the hand can act, says so first), with **End run** and **Keep climbing** (Keep delving). When the hero falls in a fight, the run waits at 0 HP with the hand paused, and the dialog, headed **Fallen in combat**, names the monster. It must be answered: **Undo** (once Rehearsed steps has unlocked undo, while undo moves remain; without it the dialog doesn't mention undo) spends one to take the fight back and leaves the hand paused; **Accept defeat** ends the run, pays what it still owes, and returns to the forest for the next run. The dialog comes back after a refresh until it is answered. Rewards are credited as they are earned and never rolled back, so taking a fight back cannot duplicate them. Ending a run with End Run (or retiring from Settings) goes straight back to the forest once confirmed. Gold is banked the moment it is found and kept however the run ends: the stats show the Gold held, and the run's end dialog and the message after the run say how much the run found.

**Gems** are the premium currency, kept between runs like Gold. The stats show the height in their right-hand column (in the forest, the tower or delve the entrance leads to, such as Tower 2), and beside it, once a run has earned any, the Inspiration (Courage in the Delve) it has earned, its icon and number flashing each time it rises; under them the Inspiration or Courage a new best would pay, and below that the currencies held, one a line: Gems, Gold and, inside a run, its Silver (amounts over 9,999 shortened, such as 123K; the exact amount on hover). A Gem now and then lies on a floor: on the next new floor a run reaches (a Tower floor not reached before in that run, or each 10 depth of the Delve, within view ahead of the hero), on a plain tile the hero can walk to, out of the way where there is one (off the routes between the hero and the floor's items, monsters, doors and stairs, tucked into a corner or nook), so the hand's cards seldom walk the hero over it, shining over the darkness. Stepping on it, or tapping it anywhere on the board, collects it in a sparkle for 1 Gem, and the next can't appear for 30 minutes (on the real clock). A Gem left on a floor the hero leaves, or in a run that ends, is gone, and another appears 3 new floors later, again and again until one is collected (that count carries into the next run). Undo never brings a collected Gem back or takes one away. Under Log, in the forest and inside a run, in the Tower and the Delve alike, a Gem button offers **7 Gems**, marked Claim (for watching an ad, once ads are hooked up; for now pressing it pays them, in a burst of blue glints); it hides once claimed and comes back 10 minutes later. In the forest Goals stands above Log, in End Run's place. Gems buy hand slots (with Larger Hand) and Training resets: each Training row has a reset button that asks to spend 2 Gems to reset that stat to no ranks, saying how many training points it returns, and allows it only when the Gems are there. The **Shop**, from the cart button at the top of the HUD's right-hand panel or at the top right of the Upgrades, Deck and Gear pages (where its Back button returns; all three show every currency held at the top (Training points from the start, Courage once Into the depths is), Gold first, a million or more in millions, billions … with two decimals, such as 1.23M), gives 25 Gems free once a day (the day turning at 00:00 GMT on the server's clock; the button shows a dot while they wait) and sells Gem packs and three one-time packs for real money: Permanent Ad-Disable (no ads, the trainers' ×2 boost for good, ×1.5 Gold), the Special Coin Pack (×2 Gold) and the Premium Coin Pack (×3 Gold); the Gold bonuses multiply each other, and packs already bought move to the bottom of the page. The server and store are not connected yet, so only the daily Gems can be claimed for now. See [docs/SHOP.md](docs/SHOP.md).

**Fractions.** Gold, HP and the hero's and monsters' stats keep their fractions in play: a 2% ATK bonus on 1 ATK strikes for 1.02, a monster in a higher tower can pay 3.1 Gold, and potion healing raised by research keeps its half points. The screen shows whole numbers: Gold and the hero's stats rounded down, each strike, heal and reward to the nearest whole, and a monster's stats to two decimals when they aren't whole.

**Higher towers.** Beating the boss on floor 100 makes floor 100's Goals checkpoint claimable, and claiming it opens **Tower II**; floor 100's checkpoint there opens Tower III, up to Tower IX. Each tower opened opens the Delve's cave of the same number. Each one's monsters have three times the HP, ATK and DEF of the one before (Tower II's are 3 times Tower I's, Tower III's 9 times), so a hero needs about three times the ATK, DEF and HP to climb as far. Each pays more Gold: ×2 in Tower II, ×3.1 in III, ×4.3 in IV, then ×5.6, ×7, ×8.5, ×10.1 and ×11.8 in IX; and its kills pay XP times the same factor as the monsters' stats (×3 in Tower II, ×9 in III …), so the hero levels faster too. Silver stays the same, so a run buys about the same run training, while potions stay the same too (only upgrades will raise those). ATK and DEF shards raise their stat by 1 in Tower I and by 2.75 times as much in each tower after (2.75 in II, 7.56 in III, …). The floors are laid out as in Tower I, but with blue and red keys and doors from the first floor. Once a second one is open, arrows on either side of the board in the forest choose which the next run enters; its numeral glows over the doorway, and the board's subtitle names it with its Gold & XP bonus. Each keeps its own records: its highest floor, the Inspiration or Courage its new floors pay (again from its first floor) and its areas mastered and cleared, and the Goals screen shows each tower's checkpoints apart.

**Areas.** Each ten Tower floors ending at a checkpoint (1–10, 11–20 …) are an area, judged as the hero climbs onto the next area's first floor. An area climbed without taking damage in a fight (a Heart Door's toll doesn't count) is **mastered**, which lets runs warp to its checkpoint once Warp is owned; one left with no monster on any of its floors is **cleared**, which pays 10 Inspiration (areas past floor 100, which have no checkpoint yet, can be cleared but not mastered). Each pays the moment it is earned, once per area in each tower, and a chest for each (gold for mastered, silver for cleared) then stands directly in front of the hero; opening it pays nothing more but bursts into gold with *AREA MASTERED* and *Checkpoint Warp Unlocked*, or *ENEMIES CLEARED* and +10 Inspiration. Undo never takes an area's reward back.

**Goals.** In the forest, the Tower's **Goals** button (with a red dot while a reward in an open tower waits to be claimed) opens the Goals screen: the tower drawn as a stone column rising from the ground, a line up its middle lit to the highest floor completed in it (shown at the bottom, *Floor 35*; a floor counts as completed once its stairs are climbed, so a tower not yet climbed shows *Floor 0*), and a checkpoint every ten floors to 100. Each checkpoint has a reward on the left, claimed by tapping it once its floor is completed (floor 10's once floor 11 is reached), and a premium reward on the right, claimed the same way with that set's **Premium Pass**: Pass 1 opens Towers I–III ($9.99), Pass 2 IV–VI ($19.99), Pass 3 VII–IX ($29.99); the pass button at the top right, or a premium reward tapped without it, offers the pass with the Gems and other rewards it opens. A reward whose floor isn't completed yet shows faded with a lock, a claimed one faded with a check, one ready to claim bright. Tower I's first six checkpoints unlock tools. Before floor 10's **Damage Prediction**, a monster's inspect panel shows only its HP, ATK and DEF; after it, also the HP the fight would cost and whether the hero survives it ("26 damage · Survivable", "0 damage · Invincible" when it costs no HP, "LETHAL", or "Instakill" when the hero's first strike fells it). Floor 20's **Combat Forecast** adds a line saying how many of the hero's hits defeat it ("5 hits to defeat", or "Instakill" when Damage Prediction isn't owned to say so); floor 30's **Attack Lore** adds another with the fewest whole points of ATK more that would defeat it in one hit fewer ("+3 ATK: 4 hits"); and floor 40's **Warp**: tapping the floor number of a checkpoint whose area is mastered (they glow, with a golden swirl at their left) then asks to begin a new run at once on the floor just above it (floor 31 for the floor 30 checkpoint), at full HP; tapping one not mastered says what warping takes. Floor 50's **Damage Visual** draws that cost in the lower-left corner of every monster on the board, without tapping it, shortened to fit (1.2K): red when lethal (∞ when the hero can't hurt it), a gray 0 for an Instakill, a white 0 when the monster strikes but takes no HP, gold otherwise; a Settings row, *Show damage on enemies*, turns it off. Floor 60's **Equipment** opens the Blacksmith (above). Tower II's floor 10 checkpoint, **Relative Damage Color**, then colours those numbers by the share of the hero's current HP each fight would cost: bright green below 1%, sliding through yellow at 10% and orange at 25% to red at 50% or more (an Instakill stays gray, a lethal fight red). Floor 100's checkpoint in Towers I to VIII unlocks the next tower (and the Delve's cave of the same number); beating floor 100's boss makes it claimable. Tapping a claimed unlock shows what it does again. A green check beside a checkpoint marks its area cleared. Entering the tower from the forest always starts on floor 1. Every tower has its own checkpoints; once a second is open, arrows at the bottom pan between them. Their premium rewards are 10, 15, 25, 35 and 50 Gems; the rewards past them are placeholders for now (Gold on the left, Gems on the right); the store isn't connected yet, so only Dev mode (or its free purchases) unlocks a pass. The Delve has no Goals yet. End Run shows only inside a run.

**Revive** (10 Inspiration, below Shroud) gives each monster's strike that would fell the hero a 0.5% chance to raise it at full HP instead, in a smaller golden blaze like a level-up's: the fight goes on from the next round, the monster as hurt and as fierce as it was, and a later strike that would fell the hero again rolls again. It opens Revive training, 1 training point a rank, each adding 0.5%, up to 50% at 99 ranks. Each strike's roll is fixed for that run, floor and tile, so undoing a fight and fighting it again ends the same way. Forecasts, Automove and the hand still treat such a fight as lost.

Rewards are credited as you climb (Courage for every 10 Delve height, Inspiration for each Tower floor completed beyond your highest, paid as you climb its stairs, and 10 for each area cleared; past floor 100, or depth 1,000, a point of either takes 10 floors, past floor 1,000 100 floors, and past floor 10,000 they stop), so retiring from Settings simply starts a fresh run. A run still in the forest takes everything bought there at once (skills, training, gear, provisions and research); skills bought while another run is inside apply to that run from its next ascent; Wayfinder, Revive, and undo-capacity upgrades unlock immediately. In the forest, Automove still avoids lethal fights; it and the hand pause off the board, in dialogs, and when hidden. No offline progress is calculated.

Inside a run, the row of tabs at the bottom (Tower, Delve, Deck, Defend, Gear, Upgrades, Settings) is hidden and its space left empty: modes, gear and upgrades are chosen in the forest before a run, and a run ends back there. The Settings button in the stats opens the Settings page at any time, with a Back button to the board. **Movement Speed** (the Courage tree's first skill, 1 Courage) opens the Archives' **Movement Speed** research (six levels, priced and timed like Focus Count's first six: 500 Gold and 8 hours, up to 8,000 Gold and 48 hours), each level letting the run's speed arrows go one step a second faster: 4, then 5, up to 9. **Rush** (1 Courage, after Movement Speed, right of Focus) opens the Archives' **Rush** research: each of its 25 levels lets the hand's first step toward a new target rush across one more empty tile in the same step (one turn, one undo), stopping on the tile before anything else (an item, a door, a monster, the stairs, a torch or a Gem); faint echoes of the hero appear on each tile it rushed off, one after another, and fade in the same order within half a second. Level n costs 250 × n × 2^(n−1) Gold and takes 1.75 × n × 1.2^(n−1) hours (250 Gold and 1.75 hours first; about 105 billion Gold and 145 days at level 25). Instant Combat likewise adds **Animate fights** (see Fights). Beside Dev mode, **Dev: free purchases** is a lighter tool for testing: it unlocks nothing, but every purchase (skills, Training, provisions, crafting, research and archivists, Defend) goes through whatever you have and costs nothing, and research completes the moment it starts. The button beside Settings is **Enter** in the forest, an arrow going into an arch: one press goes straight in through the entrance and starts the run. Inside a run it plays and pauses the hand.


## How it's built

Three modes share one page: **Tower** (compact 17 × 17 puzzle floors, generated strategy-first like a Magic Tower), **Delve** (one endless, wrapping labyrinth), and **Defend** (a city-defense simulation). Worlds are generated deterministically from the run seed, and only your changes to them are saved. The viewport shows 17 × 17 tiles with the entrance at the bottom center (20 × 20 in the forest outside); in the Delve, terrain below the last milestone gate can't be revisited.

Code lives in `src/`, with the Tower generator and its floors in `src/tower/`, the Delve labyrinth and its board in `src/delve/`, Defend in `src/defend/`, the Shop's offers, entitlements and transactions in `src/shop/`, Equipment (its catalogue, balance, inventory, effect slots, drops and Gem pulls) in `src/equipment/`, and the pages, HUD and dialogs in `src/ui/`. Design documents are in `docs/`. Architecture notes, generation invariants, and development commands for contributors (human or AI) are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Prototype boundaries

Room variety and balancing are introductory. Locked passages gate both ascent routes and reward branches; enemies inside chambers are often avoidable. Fights play out unless Animate fights is turned off. Automation prioritizes nearby upgrades and safe ascent, rather than globally optimal inventory planning. Very old tower sections cannot be revisited. Unsupported save-format versions reset safely. Layout-version migration retains progression, stats, and inventory, clears old map edits, and relocates the player to their current section entrance. All text uses the bundled variable Cinzel font from `assets/fonts/Cinzel/`; no remote font service is used. Weather is the only sound. The Archives' research is the only progression that runs while the game is closed; there is no cloud save or installable PWA.
