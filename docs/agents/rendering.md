# Rendering

**Default shared PNG kit.** `sprites/style.ts` selects the native kit for Classic; alternate Game themes retain their own renderers.
`BoardLook.styledSprites` passes this choice to tiles, pickups, all named enemies, four-direction heroes, torches, rising rewards and forest buildings.
`sprites/options.ts` provides six full-tile square door PNGs and a four-frame torch strip, controlled by the saved `squareDoors` and `spriteAnimations` settings only while the effective Game kit is active.
Square doors retain live key and HP requirement marks, including mixed locks; startup preloading prevents gameplay from starting with missing PNGs.
Torch frames share the original stationary hardware, advance at 130 milliseconds with spatial phase, and freeze when Reduce motion is on.
The water reflection style key includes both options so warmed snapshots refresh immediately on a toggle.
`sprites/animations.ts` adds native PNG potion bubbles, rare key glints, four-direction hero idle frames and the skull-and-crossbones death strip.
Ambient motion requires the effective Classic Game kit, Animations on and Reduce motion off; potion/key bodies stay static and lifted reward icons omit their ambient overlays.
Coordinate-phased potion windows last 900 milliseconds every 4.2 seconds; key glints last 600 milliseconds every 22–36 seconds, with no gameplay RNG draws or saved animation state.
`HeroIdleAnimation`, owned by the Renderer, waits 700 milliseconds after the living hero stops and then selects slow torso/inner-cape frames, resetting on movement, queued routes, combat, facing, board identity or settings changes.
The original helmet, equipment, feet and alpha silhouette remain exact in every exported idle frame, so cached shadows and directional light masks still match; dash echoes keep their static pose.
`FrameContext.heroIdleFrame` keeps the visible hero and its water image on the same frame, while pickup and hero frame keys refresh only the affected reflection bakes and snapshots.
`drawStruckDown` uses a palette-matched six-frame PNG strip in the active kit, or its first frame with Animations off or Reduce motion on, preserving the existing death timing and opacity envelope.
The death PNG stays at native scale without the old procedural bob, pulsing glow or resizing; other Game themes retain their own death mark.
`tools/prepare-ambient-sprites.mjs` exports the original effect, hero-idle and skull source sheets, registers torso changes against the existing hero and restores its original alpha before packing.
Tower and Delve have no legacy tileset or procedural Classic fallback.
`spriteRevision()` participates in ground cache keys so isolated renderers can invalidate warmed drawing caches.
`src/sprites/combat.ts` names native projectile, rank/trait, chest-star and shield pieces, exported by `tools/prepare-combat-sprites.mjs` from the combat atlases.
Enemy bakes clear on kit image arrival so small marks loaded after the body replace their procedural fallbacks.
`render/volley-art.ts` selects one of eight PNG arrow facings without changing flight positions; enemy shots use an eight-pixel orb.
`render/shield-art.ts` selects a 48-, 96- or 144-pixel hollow ring nearest the native diameter, drawing at the exact existing radius with no baked glow.
`render/chest-marks.ts` keeps one blue Supply star and two purple Equipment stars over both closed and opened chest tiles.
A Golden Floor (`BoardLook.golden`) gilds Classic's open ground as the ground cache bakes it (`gild`, `GOLDEN_GROUND` in `tile-painters.ts`) and each Game theme's ground or walls after its `ground` (`GOLDEN_LOOKS` in `themed-boards.ts`); see environments.md.
Each effect keeps its original renderer until its PNG is ready and when the effective Game kit is disabled.
`sprites/board-marks.ts` supplies native Target/Ignore (16 pixels) and skipped/blocked X (12 pixels) PNGs inside the same 24-pixel tile grid, preserving blocked-mark fading.
`boardFont` in `sprites/font.ts` selects the original bundled Tower Pixel face for Classic kit annotations, damage/reward numbers, level-up text and forest signs; other Game themes preserve the original font strings.
`EntityLighting` paints silhouettes from the active PNG contents and hero facing; both shadow and highlight caches clear immediately when the effective style or image revision changes.
`sprites/pixel-outline.ts` generates cached one-native-pixel exterior contours from sprite alpha: shaded gold for pickups/chests, blue for heroes/stairs/one-way markers and black for standard and square doors.
An exterior-air flood fill leaves interior holes and all original texels untouched, including translucent pixels; three hard palette colors follow the kit's upper-left light and nearest-neighbor scaling.
Only world painters request the contours, so inventory/control icons retain their original art; the hero's existing idle-frame alpha invariant lets all its frames share one contour per facing.
`TileArt.outline: false` and `paintHero`'s fourth argument omit decorative contours when `EntityLighting` builds physical silhouettes and torch highlights, preserving their original size and light response.
Other Game themes keep their own drawing and portrait art; changing UI themes affects only the interface kit.
`sprites/environment.ts` names sixteen native environmental components: seven 24-pixel material tiles, four 8-pixel flowers, two 8-pixel vine modules and three 12-pixel glowing mushroom clusters.
`environmentPixels` decodes material and vine PNGs once; `DecorBaker` samples their literal texels through the existing moss and pool masks, preserving coverage and bank/shore/depth selection.
Vine leaves attach to the seeded stem joints, clipped to their tile and out of water; stems remain one-pixel connectors.
Flowers and glowing caps replace their procedural bodies only after loading, and their emitted pixels and light colors follow the active PNGs.
The light-color cache includes effective style and image revision, while source plans, light geometry, water motion and interactions stay unchanged.
`sprites/forest.ts` defines twenty native forest components and two dedicated entrance bases, exported from the forest source atlases by `tools/prepare-forest-sprites.mjs`.
The Tower base is 216 × 216 (nine tiles wide), the mountain base 360 × 240 (fifteen tiles wide); their bottom centers meet the existing stairs tile, and their cropped tops continue beyond the northern view without a complete roof or peak.
`drawEntrance` uses these bases at native density, retaining the large original procedural entrance while a PNG loads or fails; the small building icons remain for interface uses.
The startup barrier warms every forest material/object PNG; `drawForestTile` draws quiet ground-only grass, litter and path PNGs, with canopies placed separately.
`forest-foliage.ts` plans coordinate-hashed understory and mature oak, elder, birch and pine crowns at 48–96 native pixels across multiple tiles, without consuming a game RNG or changing tiles.
The full rectangular extent of every object clears all roads, the mode sign, entrance footprint and both building yards; crowns may overhang ordinary woodland floor.
Plans cache by seed, mode, entrance column and road/building flags, with north-to-south ordering inside each layer and every large crown above smaller foliage.
The active forest draws dynamic back grass, small foliage, crowns, buildings, route and hero, then the existing grass at the hero's feet and weather; dynamic grass retains its original planning, motion and interaction.
Wall foliage remains with decoration off, while optional floor plants and dynamic grass follow that setting.
The forest always uses the native kit; alternate Game themes keep their own hero art, and UI themes gate the interface independently.
`sprites/dungeon.ts` supplies dedicated interior PNG materials: fitted Tower masonry and contiguous Delve stone/earth patches, selected with coordinate hashes independent of generation.
Twenty-one 96 × 720 atlases contain four 24-pixel floors, six versions of all sixteen wall-neighbor masks, four native six-pixel corners, six full tops, six dark tops and four reserved core frames: ten biome ramps for masonry, ten for rough stone and one brown earth set.
Tower floors instead use ten dedicated 96 × 96 `dungeon-floor-masonry-*` PNG panels that mix large slabs and smaller bricks across a connected 4 × 4 tile field.
`towerFloorFrame` selects neighboring frames by world coordinates and a seeded phase, reversing world y into screen rows and wrapping negative coordinates correctly without rotating the upper-left light.
The complete panel loads with its material group, and floor relief samples the same selected PNG texels; Delve retains its existing material floor variants.
`tools/prepare-tower-floors.mjs` exports the generated `assets/game-sprites/tower-floor.png` into four quiet shared-palette colors per biome, preserving the full field instead of independently sampling tile textures.
Towers II and III instead each use one fixed floor-and-wall material set at every height: warm limestone ashlar and rectangular paving for II, cool blue-violet basalt and octagonal paving with small square insets for III.
`towerTileset` selects only these two numbered Tower tiers; Tower I, later tiers, Delve, forests, non-Classic Game themes keep their existing art.
Each set has one 96 × 96 connected floor PNG and one 96 × 720 wall PNG with the existing six variants, sixteen neighbor masks, native corners and shade frames.
`tools/prepare-tower-tier-sprites.mjs` extracts the original `assets/game-sprites/tower-2-materials.png` and `tower-3-materials.png` into the shared palette with fixed upper-left lighting.
The wall-corner alpha footprints are shared, while visible corner texels come from the new material; these tiers omit the older biome-specific wall motifs.
`BoardLook.tier` passes the run's saved number into `TerrainTile`, material selection and floor relief; ground, native wall, relief and water-reflection caches include the effective tileset so a reused renderer switches immediately.
The shared contact shadows and continuous shade contours remain in place, and both new wall sets recede to the same flat black core.
`dungeonWallVariant` chooses six authored top and south-facing facade pairs by coordinate hash, so long connected walls have seeded variety without rotating their lighting or altering geometry.
Masonry varies broad fitted blocks and worn joints; bedrock uses continuous quiet planes with sparse fractures, while earth varies compact layers and embedded stone.
A shared 96 × 216 atlas retains the original contact-shadow masks and soil reference pieces.
`dungeon-blends.png` supplies four variants of sixteen cardinal masks and four diagonal patches each (96 × 480), with stepped binary-alpha contours and clear centers.
`dungeonMaterialJoins` overlays the neighboring earth PNG through those masks on both floors and exposed stone walls, reusing the selected frame and preserving blocked boundaries.
Masked material bakes are cached with a 512-entry limit; no gradients, soft alpha or runtime color interpolation are used.
`dungeonDiagonals` reads the four actual diagonal tiles only in the active kit; the overlays address northwest, northeast, southeast and southwest inside their existing collision tile.
Wall corners draw only where a diagonal opening meets two joined or two exposed sides; straight edges and missing diagonal terrain retain their existing lips.
Diagonal material patches draw where earth touches stone at a corner without an existing cardinal blend or a change between open floor and blocking wall.
Walls expose shallow three-pixel north/side lips and eight-pixel south-facing facades, keeping a steep top-down angle inside the existing collision tile; joined sides have no facade.
Exposed wall texture recedes through three hard shade levels, with connected two-pixel contours and a broad material body.
The shadow body continues beyond the bright rim, so thin walls remain solid and wider walls recede through a fuller intermediate shade before black.
`render/wall-depth.ts` records actual openings in a 5 × 5 neighborhood, treating neighboring air as exposed wall, second-ring air as transition and farther solids as core.
`dungeonWallBands` measures native pixel distance to those air rectangles, preserving the position of diagonal openings instead of illuminating two entire edges.
Every material counts as connected solid, missing cells remain solid, and doors/items/stairs count as air; generation and collision are unchanged.
The contour thresholds lie at 14, 28 and 42 native pixels from air, with a small coordinate-hashed displacement interpolated over 48-pixel world cells to avoid repeating tile-shaped cutouts.
Binary clipping selects literal PNG texels from full tops at `DUNGEON_CAP_FRAME`, the existing shaded wall body and dark tops at `DUNGEON_SHADE_FRAME`; only geometry varies, never color interpolation or lighting direction.
Physical coping and south-facing facades draw separately over the shade bands, retaining their original height and upper-left lighting.
Each complete wall is composed once at native 24 × 24 resolution before nearest-neighbor scaling, so fractional ground-cache transforms cannot antialias the internal clipping boundaries.
Wall-image bakes have a 512-entry cache keyed by art, seed, coordinates and actual opening geometry.
Material blending continues through the shaded band as well as exposed wall tops.
`render/dungeon-details.ts` adapts Celeste-style neighborhood rules and optional overlap sprites into sparse, original 48 × 24 wall motifs shared across two collision tiles.
`dungeonDetail` chooses one candidate row per seeded 3 × 3 block, keeps a clear column between motifs, and rejects missing cells, material seams, incompatible north/south edges and pairs without nearby openings.
Thin strips select small chips before general rules; sheltered side runs select inset joints, while broad exposed edges choose three fracture/joint variants without rotating the lighting.
The ten masonry `dungeon-details-*` PNGs are 336 × 48 with seven motifs; the ten stone and one earth atlases remain 192 × 48 with four motifs, each in bright/body shades matching the base biome ramps.
Tower-only repaired mortar joints join broad-edge variants, spalled flakes alternate with chips on thin strips, and chisel grooves alternate with inset joints on sheltered faces, preserving the existing seeded spacing.
Details compose inside native wall bakes after each surface pass, clipped to the first two shade bands and covered by the original coping/facades and corner pieces; deep shade and flat black cores stay quiet.
Optional detail images load independently: a missing image preserves the complete existing native wall, and a late image changes the bake key and sprite revision so warmed caches repaint.
`tools/prepare-dungeon-details.mjs` exports the original `assets/game-sprites/dungeon-details.png` and Tower extension `assets/game-sprites/tower-masonry-details.png`; both dungeon exporters use `tools/dungeon-palette.mjs` for identical biome colors.
`dungeon-infill.png` retains the common core and earlier sparse reference pieces (96 × 96); the deep core stays flat and is never biome-tinted.
The styled decor bake skips core walls so moss cannot repaint their flat interiors; alternate Game themes keep their own environment.
`paintGround` returns after drawing these native tiles, so old procedural quoins cannot add off-palette corners over the new walls.
Startup preloads every material group, and `spriteRevision` also refreshes ground caches in isolated renderer tests.
`dungeonFloorSprite` extracts each selected native floor frame once for first-section torch relief; `render/floor-relief.ts` accepts image or canvas sources, and `LightingPass` invalidates its torch bakes when kit style or image revision changes.
Changing Game themes on a live renderer therefore restores matching terrain and floor relief; all other lighting and decor retain their existing placement and behavior.
The water mirror receives the effective board-art key too, refreshing cached walls, hero and torch snapshots immediately when sprites or board art change.
`tools/prepare-dungeon-sprites.mjs` extracts the material components, assembles connected walls and edge masks, and maps all twenty biome variations into the existing palette.
Its corner components come from `assets/game-sprites/dungeon-corners.png`, sharing the existing native grid, upper-left light and restricted material ramps.
Its six top/facade pairs per material come from `assets/game-sprites/dungeon-wall-variations.png`; floor, coping and corner components retain their existing sources.
The shared core and stepped blend masks come from `assets/game-sprites/dungeon-material-blends.png`.

Board drawing (`Renderer`, frames, lighting, decor, popups, tile caches, the forest outside, weather), the render goldens, and the Medieval and Neon themes.
Board drawing lives in `src/render/`: the Renderer, its frame, camera, tile cache, terrain painters, tilesets and lighting at its root; the Game themes' boards (`*-board.ts`, `themed-board.ts`, `themed-boards.ts`, `themed-art.ts`, `ascii-glyphs.ts`) in `src/render/boards/`; decor (`decor*.ts`, `forest-foliage.ts`, `outside-grass.ts`) in `src/render/decor/`; and the passing effects (poofs, explosions, flashes, glows, damage labels, particles) in `src/render/effects/`.
The sprite modules (the PNG kit, `sprites/kit.ts`, and every `sprites/*.ts` it feeds, with the board's and the interface's sprites) are in `src/sprites/`, since the interface uses them as much as the board.

**Rendering layers.** `render/rendering.ts` (`Renderer`) owns the camera and smooth player interpolation: the camera, and the hero outside a run's turns, ease after their targets, while through a turn's single step inside a run (`Game.turnShown`) the hero walks at an even pace over the turn's `ms`, so it stands where that turn's shots leave from (a turn stamped after the first frame showing it walks from that frame).
Inside the Delve the camera is `FollowCamera` (`src/render/camera.ts`, no drawing), which trails where the hero is drawn rather than its tile.
It holds still while the hero stays within a dead zone round the middle (`DEAD_ZONE`, 2 tiles each way), so a step or two and back never pans; past it, the camera's goal follows just far enough to keep the hero at the zone's edge.
The camera glides to its goal as a critically damped spring (`GLIDE_SECONDS`, `FAST_GLIDE_SECONDS` with Fast transitions), easing in and out with no jolt; its glide quickens with the hero's recent pace, so its trail fits between the dead zone and `EDGE_MARGIN`, the 3 tiles always kept between the hero and the view's edge.
With a path ahead (`Game.pathAhead`: a tapped walk, Target's path, or the hand's card's, nearest step first), the camera frames the path instead: `pointsAhead` adds up the next `LOOK_AHEAD` (8) steps' moves from the hero's tile, so a path round the wrap stays beside it, and the goal moves as little as it can to keep the hero within `EDGE_MARGIN` plus a tile and as many of those steps, in order, `AHEAD_MARGIN` (2 tiles) inside the view.
A trip out and back that fits never pans, and nearing a path's end the camera stops at the end rather than showing past it; it keeps that framing for `PATH_GRACE_SECONDS` after a path ends, through the moment the next card chooses its path, before the dead zone takes over again.
A dash puts the hero there at once, so the renderer calls `FollowCamera.leap` (and any move of more than `LEAP_TILES`, 1.5, in a frame counts as one): until the hero is back within `EDGE_MARGIN`, the camera only glides, ignoring the leap's speed and holding the hero nowhere, so however long or often the hero dashes it never jumps, and the hero may stand near or past the view's edge for a moment.
Reduce Motion and Instant transitions stand it at its goal at once; `Renderer.target` reports the goal, and `isIdle` waits for it to settle.
`tests/camera.test.ts` covers it.
`CameraView.height` optionally bounds the vertical axis for finite rooms, with optional `padding` for overlaid controls; without it the Delve retains its open-ended northward view.
Rooms smaller than the viewport center within it on either bounded axis.
The turns' shots (`render/volley-art.ts`): `ShotsInFlight` keeps every volley still flying (a turn's last shots land as the next's leave), and `drawVolley` starts each on the display frame nearest its tick (`shotFlight`: up to half a frame early, a tie going to the later frame), an arrow from where the hero stood as it fired, an enemy's shot homing on the hero as drawn.
An enemy an arrow felled is cleared from the board at once, but `ShotsInFlight.board` (the frame's `f.world`, one object per board) keeps it standing on its tile until that arrow lands, for every pass that reads the frame's tiles.
Each `draw()` builds one `FrameContext` (`render/render-frame.ts`: the camera's `BoardView`, the canvas, the board's tiles as a narrow `BoardTiles` rather than the `Game`, the `BoardLook`, and the torches, walls and glows in view).
`BoardView` (`left`, `bottom`, `n`, `s`) is the one view shape: the tile caches, decor and the forest grass all take the frame itself as their view.
`BoardLook` holds the art set (mode, tier, height, seed, outside) and the player's settings (sprites off, reduced motion), and decides once per frame whether the board is area one (`area1`, the first Tower section's hand-drawn art and floor relief); passes read these from `f.look`, never from the game and draws the ground over it.
Outside, it then draws the tile contents, entrance, route line, hero in its grass, and weather; in the dungeon, `DungeonLight` (`render/dungeon-light.ts`) runs the rest of the frame in its fixed order (torch relief, decor, cast shadows, darkness and glows, tile contents, torches and lightmap, route line, hero and what stands in front of it, frame edge, vignette), calling back into a `LitBoard` the renderer builds for what it draws between the light's steps.
What the game hands the board to show of its moves (`Gain`, `Heal`, `Facing`, `Dash`, `ShownFight`, `Encounter`) is typed in `shown.ts`, so the renderer and themed boards draw them without importing `state.ts`.
Blocked-step and feedback text, then the skull over a hero struck down (`render/effects/struck-down.ts`), the revival's fire and the level-up burst (`render/effects/level-up.ts`), go last.
The passes live in their own modules.
`render/tile-painters.ts` holds the tile art (one painter per tile kind, plus the hero and torch sprites), and every painter draws onto the context it's given.
A wall tile gets a corner brick (`drawCornerBricks`) in each inner room corner only where its art runs a mortar edge for the brick to meet: the area1 sprites and the procedural walls; the other biomes' wall sprites go without, and only the area1 sprites wrap a quoin round their outer corners (`drawArea1Quoins`).
Items, the torch and the hero wear a coloured outline (`withOutline`); an enemy is baked once per name, tier, `strength` and whether it is Fast or Ranged (`bakeEnemy`, an arrow at the top right for Ranged, the Fast mark below it when both: a one-pixel black outline, then `ENEMY_LOOK`'s bands outside it and rank chevrons over it (`rankChevrons` in `render/rank-chevrons.ts`, `RANK_CHEVRONS`: a weak enemy's one pointing down, one for strong, two for elite, three for a boss, four for a Greater Boss), and a Fast enemy's cyan fast-forward mark at the top right, `fastMark`; the Neon enemy draws one too), and its dark-room glow in `render/lighting.ts` is keyed by strength too.
Only opaque bands cast torch shadows.
Ground uses the dedicated native dungeon PNGs through `render/tile-painters.ts`.
`render/themes.ts` retains biome palette selection and neighbor metadata; the procedural Classic terrain renderer and old area-one/themed tileset loaders are removed.
`DungeonLight` (two methods: `glows(f)` starts a frame, `draw(f, board)` runs it) is the renderer's only entry point to the lighting, and owns the hand-offs between its two passes (the shadow layer, the sprite darkness, the clip regions around contents, hero and foreground): `render/lighting-pass.ts` (`LightingPass`) handles the Brightness darkness, object glows, torch lightmap and haze, floor relief, and vignette, and `render/entity-lighting.ts` (`EntityLighting`) handles torch-cast sprite shadows and directional light on sprites.
`render/lighting.ts` holds the lighting settings and torch flicker and sway; each torch's visibility polygon comes from `torches.ts`.
`tests/lighting-passes.test.ts` drives `DungeonLight` over seeded Tower and Delve frames (with a board that paints tile contents, the hero and assorted foregrounds) on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call (and read back pixels made up from what was drawn), runs the darkening pass alone over odd clip regions, and hashes the calls along with visibility polygons, flicker and sway against `tests/fixtures/lighting-passes.golden.json`.
Each scene numbers the canvases it makes from zero and names ones left over from earlier scenes by size, so a change to one scene doesn't shift the hashes of the others; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended lighting change.
`render/route-path.ts` draws the golden route line along `Game.shownPath`: a tapped walk, or the hand's card's committed path (`cardPlan`) while a pressed card (`run.focused`) leads it, or, with Auto-Climb and the `showPath` setting (the Toggles menu's *Show path*, shown once Auto-Climb is owned), whichever card leads; it shrinks behind the hero as `cardPlan.path` is stepped off.
Floor relief stays shallow because the diffuse floor sprites already shade their grout; directional masks extend boundary pixels instead of inventing a groove beyond every sprite's bounds (`render/floor-relief.ts`).
`render/shield-art.ts` (`drawShields`, first of the renderer's overlays, on every board and Game theme, above the dark) draws each standing Shielded enemy's bubble (`Board.shields`, enemies/shielded.ts): a glowing green ring of its radius with a faint fill, centred on its tile, also by the other edge in the Delve; the render scene `towerShield` captures the original look and `spriteCombat` the optional PNG boundary.
`render/chest-marks.ts` (`drawChestMarks`, next among the overlays, above the dark) marks each Supply and Equipment Chest in view, and the floor each leaves once opened (`Tile.chest`): a light blue or purple glow over its tile and one or two stars in its upper-left corner (`CHEST_LOOK`, whose colours `render/lighting-pass.ts` also gives its glow).
`render/effects/boss-flash.ts` (`BossFlash`, from the renderer's overlays) pulses a golden-red glow and frame on each boss the first time it is in view in a run, so a boss floor is noticed on arrival.
`render/board-popups.ts` (`BoardPopups`) draws what rises off the board above everything else: each strike's damage number as it lands (a darker red on the hero, and what the shroud blocked in blue beside it; an enemy strike's or shot's thorns in yellow over the enemy as it lands, none in summary rounds, where they would only cover the drops; every number through `popupAmount` in `whole.ts`, whole, then from 100,000 to three figures as `shortAmount` gives them), the HP each potion or Lifesteal heals as a blue `+N` over the hero, and each gain in turn (one second each, fading for the last quarter; a tile's contents through `paintContents`, an Equipment material (with its count) or piece (with its rarity's mark) as its pixel icon from `ui/equipment-icons.ts`, the amount of Gold or Silver beside its coin, or the gain's text when it has no sprite or it hasn't loaded), and `lunges` leans the hero (through the frame's `playerX`/`playerY`, so its light and shadow follow) and the enemy into their strikes.
A strike marked `pronounced` (a Surprise Attack's first, a Power Strike's) wears a Charged Shot's full gold outline.
A fight's `opening` (Takedown, Tangle) draws over the enemy until its first strike swings (`drawOpening`): Tangle's glowing blue down arrow, then Takedown's X of two glowing slashes over it, shrinking from `SLASH_START` times its size; summary rounds show none.
A chest's Double or Triple raises a `{ multiplier }` gain that `drawFlash` draws large and in place, pulsing gold, for the gain's second.
Each popup starts the moment it happens, over any still rising (gains that came together one after another, except a kill's or a chest's drops, `Gain.drop` from `Finds`' `drops`, which start together, each its `row` of `DROP_ROW`, 0.7 tiles, from the tile: a kill's Gold over its Silver, at the tile, with Cells and Equipment's finds below; a chest's Gold at the tile, its materials below; a played-out fight's drops hold until `DROP_HOLD_MS` after its last strike lands, as its last numbers begin to fade, so they never rise through them), and they draw oldest first so newer ones sit on top, every number over every gain's sprite but the drops, so a potion's heal never hides behind the item rising from the same tile, and the drops over every number, so a kill's finds never hide behind its damage.
`render/effects/position-lights.ts` draws, among the overlays after the chest marks and so above the darkness on every board, inside a run only: a cool breathing light round each Tower staircase in view (`drawStairsLights`), and a warm light round the hero's tile wherever it is drawn (`drawHeroLight`), with a golden pulse (`WAIT_PULSE_MS`, steady when motion is reduced) while `Game.awaitsPress` (before Auto-Climb, the hero waiting for a card press with no path to follow).
Each is a ring of light hollow at its middle, so the sprite stays clear; a frame costs one gradient fill per light.
These position rings, the cool hero darkness halo, staircase emission, provision boost rings and floor-arrival flash are temporarily disabled together by `POSITION_HALOS_ENABLED = false` in `render/lighting.ts`.
The switch preserves their implementations for restoration without affecting native blue player/stair contours, torch lighting or combat feedback.
Disabled arrival and boost glows do not keep idle frames running.
When the hero reaches another Tower floor, the renderer snaps it onto its new tile instead of gliding and fades a white glow behind it (the `LitBoard`'s optional `halo`, which `DungeonLight` draws after the darkness and before the hero, so it is never dimmed; the renderer gives it only while the glow lasts).
The board's fixed seeded scenes and their frame timings live in `tests/render-scenes.ts`, shared by two goldens.
Each scene's seed is its place in the job list, so a new board scene goes in `LATER_BOARD_SCENES`, after all the others, to keep every existing scene's seed.
`tests/render-calls.test.ts` (part of `npm test`, no browser needed) draws them in Node on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call, with images loaded at their real sizes, and hashes the calls at each captured frame against `tests/fixtures/render-calls.golden.json`.
It is the same on every machine, so it is the main check that a rendering refactor draws exactly the same.
`Math.pow` comes from the system C library and can differ in its last bit between Linux (CI) and Windows, so this test and the lighting golden import `tests/portable-math.ts` first, which swaps in a `pow` built from V8's own `exp` and `log`; a new golden whose hashes pass through `pow` should do the same; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to what the board draws.
`tests/render-golden.mjs` (`npm run test:render`) draws the same scenes in a browser and compares pixel hashes against `tests/fixtures/render.golden.json`.
It hashes a scene only once every image the app asked for has loaded and a capture asks for no more, fails if the scene then draws differently twice (checked for every scene when recording, otherwise only for a scene that no longer matches), and keeps every canvas on the CPU (Chrome otherwise moves a canvas there partway through a run after enough pixel reads, and the two paths blend differently), so repeated runs agree exactly.
Pixels can still differ between browsers and machines, so before changing rendering, regenerate the golden on the unchanged code with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`, then compare after your change (PNGs of the frames that changed go to `test-results/render/`, the recorded ones to `test-results/render-golden/`).
The viewport is a fixed 17×17 tiles (`VIEWPORT_TILES`; 20×20 outside); rendering density is independent of world dimensions.
`render/tile-cache.ts` bakes rarely-changing layers (floor, decor pixels) into one offscreen image per layer covering the view plus a margin, and repaints only when the view leaves that area, the board or tile size changes, or tile art is still loading.
Battery saver drops to 30 fps while nothing moves.
`node tools/transition-bench.mjs` times the board transitions (a Tower climb, the Delve walked up through new chunks and areas, going in from the forest) in Edge with the CPU slowed 4× (`THROTTLE`): the step's logic, the first frame after it and the frames catching up after that; `PROFILE=1` lists where each scenario's time went, `ONLY=tower` runs one.
Compare its numbers before and after a change, on one machine.
`decor-render.ts` (`DecorLayer`, the renderer's only decor entry point) budgets tile planning per frame and runs decor's passes: `decor-bake.ts` bakes each tile's static pixels for the tile cache, `decor-effects.ts` owns the live state and particle physics (splintering crates, stirred grass, spores, splashes and ripples), `decor-reflections.ts` mirrors crates, walls, tile contents, torch flames and the hero into pools (the renderer hands `drawGround` a `MirroredSprites` that paints each in tile space; the mirror lines, snapshots and reuse live in decor), and `decor-sprites.ts` paints crates, parting grass, ripple rings and drips.
Glowing blooms and fireflies scale with the Brightness setting.
Its draw passes take one `DecorFrame` (view, time, tile lookup, reduced motion).
`tests/art-modules.test.ts` walks `DecorLayer` through seeded Tower rooms and Delve areas (crates broken, pools waded, reflections painted and reused or refreshed, a tight planning budget) on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call, and hashes that along with the Upgrades page's particle fluid (`render/effects/tree-particles.ts`, over seeded frames with resizes, purchases, the shimmering rays around a node just unlocked (`unlock`, its first rank) and reduced motion) against `tests/fixtures/art-modules.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to that art.
The render golden includes decor scenes (a crate stepped on, a pool waded into, tall grass walked through) that check the effect is live before hashing.
Lighting, weather, torch light, outside grass, and tree particles are separate modules.
`render/cloud-shadows.ts` replaces the old flat cloud gradients with a seeded, wrapping density texture projected onto a four-native-pixel height map of the visible forest sprites.
Sprite alpha silhouettes and drawing order preserve ground gaps; canopy crowns, the tower/mountain and building roofs use approximate height ramps, and the moving hero samples its own silhouette without rebuilding the static map.
The map and PNG alpha readbacks are cached by forest layout, decoration/style and sprite revision; ordinary cloud frames perform 96 × 96 bilinear samples and one scaled canvas draw, with no full-board readback or dynamic blur.
Cloud drift ticks at 15 Hz; moving the hero or camera also refreshes the projection so the shadows stay attached to the surfaces.
The same density texture supplies god rays through clear cloud gaps: sixteen samples along the sun direction are integrated once per seed into a wrapping shaft texture, then sampled into a second cached 96 × 96 warm screen-blended overlay.
`CLOUD_LIGHT.direction` sets a steep integer (1,3), roughly 72-degree direction for both ray ribbons and height projection; `elevationScale` controls the height displacement and `driftX`/`driftY` set the shared animation speed.
`GOD_RAY_STYLE` caps weather opacity at 0.16/0.13/0.045/0.02 and controls eight normalized feather layers, their 2.8× outer width, and two radius-two separable blur passes on the 128 × 128 shaft texture.
`render/soft-field.ts` provides reusable `layeredFeather` and `softenField` helpers for cached light, fog or glow density; wrap mode preserves cloud seams, while clamp mode supports local effects.
Feather neighboring bands first, then soften their combined field once per seed; no canvas filters, per-frame blur, added frame buffers or full-resolution readbacks are needed, and the original sprite art stays crisp.
The rays share cloud motion, height projection, pause and Reduce motion, weaken in rain/storms and replace the old fixed sunny triangles; ordinary frames never march rays or blur a full-size image.
The projection is anchored in forest coordinates during camera movement, covers every outdoor weather and Game theme, and runs before rain and localized lightning so those stay visible.
Weather has no full-screen overcast tint or sunny color wash; cloud shadows use neutral black attenuation and warm light is restricted to the actual feathered shafts, with no diffuse ray baseline lifting the whole clearing.
Cloud time follows `OutdoorWeather.elapsed`, freezes with pause/Reduce motion/hidden tabs and resets with the forest seed; it never draws gameplay randomness or changes collision/save data.
The Tower forest has a full 20-column movement grid, matching its viewport, independently of the 17-column dungeon; the newly reachable right-side grass uses the same seeded tree scattering as the other grass.
The path, entrance, trial square, grass clearances and shadow-height landmarks all use the mode's entrance column rather than half the clearing width, keeping the art aligned with its collision and interaction tiles.
`forest-arrival.ts` samples the fixed pause and linear sprite walk, while `FrameLoop` advances it and `Renderer` skips hero painting during the pause; native hero frames, facing and world rendering are shared with ordinary movement.
The appended `forestTravel` render job covers empty, walking, settled and reduced-motion frames in both entry directions, without changing earlier scene seeds.
Outside the entrance, `outside.ts` holds the forest clearing (`OutsideWorld`, where a forest run's hero starts on the crossroads partway up the path, `OUTSIDE_START_Y`, and whose path's foot, `atExit`, leads to the other forest; its native ground tiles and the tower and cave entrances; tiles are addressed by a `ForestSpot`), the crossroads' road (the row of `OUTSIDE_START_Y`) which continues to the Blacksmith's and the Hall's doors once each stands, and on through the trees to the left or right edge while a tier before or after the selected one is open, as the tier arrows lead (`Roads`, `roadsOf`, on `ForestSpot` and `BoardLook`; `Game.forestWorld` lays them), `render/weather.ts` (`OutdoorWeather`) times thunder and paints sun, cloud, rain and lightning from one `WeatherFrame` of settings, and loops the recorded forest wind (`blow`, `sound-files.ts`'s `windForest` bed, cross-faded into a seamless loop) while the forest shows with Weather sounds on, fading it away on going inside, leaving the board (`Renderer.hush`, from the frame loop) or hiding the page, and `render/torch-light.ts` bakes each torch's glow from a walking-distance search and a wall-aware box blur.
`tests/outdoor-art.test.ts` hashes seeded weather runs (thunder timing, sound calls, and the drawing on a recording canvas), the clearing's tiles, steps and art, and torch light fields over seeded wall maps against `tests/fixtures/outdoor-art.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to them.
The dirt road uses the shared native forest ground kit, keeping its existing walkable geometry.
The walk in through the entrance (`Game.walkIn`, set only when `Game.animatesEntrance`, which `main.ts` turns on; tests enter at once): reaching the steps (the Tower's, or the Delve's once `beginTournament` begins from them) the hero stays on them in the game, while the renderer draws it a turn later in the arch's tile above (`heroSpot`), darkening as it steps in, then not at all; then `zoomIntoEntrance` scales each drawn frame about that tile over `ENTRANCE_ZOOM_MS`, evenly in steps of size, the tile drawn toward the board's middle until it fills the board, and the run begins (`walkInTurn`), cutting to its first floor.

**Themes** come in two kinds (`src/theme-collection.ts`, `THEMES`): a UI theme dresses the page around the board (setting `uiTheme`: `classic`, `medieval`, `neon`, `terminal`, `desktop`, `glyph`, `ember`, `yang`, `yin`, `shiro`, `kuro`, `arcade`, `system`, `orbit`, `pixel`, `doodle` or `murk`), a Game theme redraws the board itself, its tiles, hero, enemies and torches (setting `gameTheme`: `classic`, `neon`, `terminal`, `desktop`, `glyph`, `ember`, `yang`, `yin`, `shiro`, `kuro`, `arcade`, `system`, `kroz`, `orbit`, `pixel`, `doodle` or `murk`).
Classic is each kind's free look; every other theme is owned once bought (`save.themes`, `decodeThemes`): Medieval (UI) from the start (`starting`), every other for `THEME_PRICE`, 1 Gold, the one price for all in one currency (`CurrencyId`, spent through `CURRENCIES`), so moving themes to another currency is that line.
`game.themes.buy` (`ThemeDesk`, `game/theme-desk.ts`) (`buyTheme`, `canBuyTheme`) pays it, nothing with free purchases on.
In Dev mode every theme is owned: `game.themes.claim`, run by `main.ts`'s `update`, gives the ones not owned yet (`grantAllThemes`) and announces each with a toast (`themeToast` in `ui/theme-menu.ts`); without Dev mode it gives none.
Each theme owned adds 2.5% to all Gold banked (`themeGoldFactor`, in `RunPurse.gold` and the Gold bonuses' list).
The look worn is `uiLook` / `gameLook`: the chosen one while owned, else Classic, so an old save that chose Neon before owning it shows Classic until it does.
A new hero starts in Medieval.
The Settings page's UI Themes and Game Themes buttons open each kind's menu (`ui/theme-menu.ts`, `renderThemeMenu`, drawn into the Settings page): a card per choice with its preview (`public/assets/themes/<kind>-<look>.png`, screenshots of the game in that look), name, Gold, and Equip (`game.themes.wear`), Equipped, or Buy with the price as the currency's icon and amount (disabled while short of it); `tests/theme-collection.test.ts` covers buying, Dev mode, ownership and the Gold.
