# Rendering

Board drawing (`Renderer`, frames, lighting, decor, popups, tile caches, the forest outside, weather), the render goldens, and the Medieval and Neon themes.

**Rendering layers.** `rendering.ts` (`Renderer`) owns the camera and smooth player interpolation.
Each `draw()` builds one `FrameContext` (`render-frame.ts`: the camera's `BoardView`, the canvas, the board's tiles as a narrow `BoardTiles` rather than the `Game`, the `BoardLook`, and the torches, walls and glows in view).
`BoardView` (`left`, `bottom`, `n`, `s`) is the one view shape: the tile caches, decor and the forest grass all take the frame itself as their view.
`BoardLook` holds the art set (mode, height, seed, outside) and the player's settings (sprites off, reduced motion), and decides once per frame whether the board is area one (`area1`, the first Tower section's hand-drawn art and floor relief); passes read these from `f.look`, never from the game and draws the ground over it.
Outside, it then draws the tile contents, entrance, route line, hero in its grass, and weather; in the dungeon, `DungeonLight` (`dungeon-light.ts`) runs the rest of the frame in its fixed order (torch relief, decor, cast shadows, darkness and glows, tile contents, torches and lightmap, route line, hero and what stands in front of it, frame edge, vignette), calling back into a `LitBoard` the renderer builds for what it draws between the light's steps.
Blocked-step and feedback text, then the level-up burst (`level-up.ts`), go last.
The passes live in their own modules.
`tile-painters.ts` holds the tile art (one painter per tile kind, plus the hero and torch sprites), and every painter draws onto the context it's given.
Items, the torch and the hero wear a coloured outline (`withOutline`); an enemy is baked once per name, tier and `strength` (`bakeEnemy`: a one-pixel black outline, then `ENEMY_LOOK`'s bands outside it and rank chevrons over it, a weak enemy's one pointing down), and its dark-room glow in `lighting.ts` is keyed by strength too.
Only opaque bands cast torch shadows.
Ground uses the PNG tilesets (`themed-tilesets.ts`) and falls back to `drawTerrain` in `themes.ts`, the procedural art (wall faces per theme, floor contact shadows, and a small library of details) shown when sprites are off or still loading.
`tests/terrain-paint.test.ts` runs `drawTerrain` on a recording canvas over every Tower theme and several Delve areas and hashes the calls against `tests/fixtures/terrain-paint.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended art change.
`DungeonLight` (two methods: `glows(f)` starts a frame, `draw(f, board)` runs it) is the renderer's only entry point to the lighting, and owns the hand-offs between its two passes (the shadow layer, the sprite darkness, the clip regions around contents, hero and foreground): `lighting-pass.ts` (`LightingPass`) handles the Brightness darkness, object glows, torch lightmap and haze, floor relief, and vignette, and `entity-lighting.ts` (`EntityLighting`) handles torch-cast sprite shadows and directional light on sprites.
`lighting.ts` holds the lighting settings and torch flicker and sway; each torch's visibility polygon comes from `torches.ts`.
`tests/lighting-passes.test.ts` drives `DungeonLight` over seeded Tower and Delve frames (with a board that paints tile contents, the hero and assorted foregrounds) on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call (and read back pixels made up from what was drawn), runs the darkening pass alone over odd clip regions, and hashes the calls along with visibility polygons, flicker and sway against `tests/fixtures/lighting-passes.golden.json`.
Each scene numbers the canvases it makes from zero and names ones left over from earlier scenes by size, so a change to one scene doesn't shift the hashes of the others; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended lighting change.
`route-path.ts` draws the golden route line.
`boss-flash.ts` (`BossFlash`, from the renderer's overlays) pulses a golden-red glow and frame on each boss the first time it is in view in a run, so a boss floor is noticed on arrival.
`board-popups.ts` (`BoardPopups`) draws what rises off the board above everything else: each strike's damage number as it lands (a darker red on the hero, and what the shroud blocked in silver beside it), the HP each potion or Lifesteal heals as a blue `+N` over the hero, and each gain in turn (one second each, fading for the last quarter; a tile's contents through `paintContents`, a material's PNG from `material-sprites.ts`, the amount of Gold or Silver beside its coin, or the gain's text when it has no sprite or it hasn't loaded), and `lunges` leans the hero (through the frame's `playerX`/`playerY`, so its light and shadow follow) and the enemy into their strikes.
Each popup starts the moment it happens, over any still rising (gains that came together, like a chest's Gold and materials, one after another), and they draw oldest first so newer ones sit on top, every number over every gain's sprite, so a potion's heal or a strike's damage never hides behind the item rising from the same tile.
When the hero reaches another Tower floor, the renderer snaps it onto its new tile instead of gliding and fades a white glow behind it (the `LitBoard`'s optional `halo`, which `DungeonLight` draws after the darkness and before the hero, so it is never dimmed; the renderer gives it only while the glow lasts).
The board's fixed seeded scenes and their frame timings live in `tests/render-scenes.ts`, shared by two goldens.
Each scene's seed is its place in the job list, so a new board scene goes in `LATER_BOARD_SCENES`, after all the others, to keep every existing scene's seed.
`tests/render-calls.test.ts` (part of `npm test`, no browser needed) draws them in Node on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call, with images loaded at their real sizes, and hashes the calls at each captured frame against `tests/fixtures/render-calls.golden.json`.
It is the same on every machine, so it is the main check that a rendering refactor draws exactly the same.
`Math.pow` comes from the system C library and can differ in its last bit between Linux (CI) and Windows, so this test and the lighting golden import `tests/portable-math.ts` first, which swaps in a `pow` built from V8's own `exp` and `log`; a new golden whose hashes pass through `pow` should do the same; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to what the board draws.
`tests/render-golden.mjs` (`npm run test:render`) draws the same scenes in a browser and compares pixel hashes against `tests/fixtures/render.golden.json`.
It hashes a scene only once every image the app asked for has loaded and a capture asks for no more, fails if the scene then draws differently twice (checked for every scene when recording, otherwise only for a scene that no longer matches), and keeps every canvas on the CPU (Chrome otherwise moves a canvas there partway through a run after enough pixel reads, and the two paths blend differently), so repeated runs agree exactly.
Pixels can still differ between browsers and machines, so before changing rendering, regenerate the golden on the unchanged code with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`, then compare after your change (PNGs of the frames that changed go to `test-results/render/`, the recorded ones to `test-results/render-golden/`).
The viewport is a fixed 17×17 tiles (`VIEWPORT_TILES`; 20×20 outside); rendering density is independent of world dimensions.
`tile-cache.ts` bakes rarely-changing layers (floor, decor pixels) into one offscreen image per layer covering the view plus a margin, and repaints only when the view leaves that area, the board or tile size changes, or tile art is still loading.
Battery saver drops to 30 fps while nothing moves.
`decor-render.ts` (`DecorLayer`, the renderer's only decor entry point) budgets tile planning per frame and runs decor's passes: `decor-bake.ts` bakes each tile's static pixels for the tile cache, `decor-effects.ts` owns the live state and particle physics (splintering crates, stirred grass, spores, splashes and ripples), `decor-reflections.ts` mirrors crates, walls, tile contents, torch flames and the hero into pools (the renderer hands `drawGround` a `MirroredSprites` that paints each in tile space; the mirror lines, snapshots and reuse live in decor), and `decor-sprites.ts` paints crates, parting grass, ripple rings and drips.
Glowing blooms and fireflies scale with the Brightness setting.
Its draw passes take one `DecorFrame` (view, time, tile lookup, reduced motion).
`tests/art-modules.test.ts` walks `DecorLayer` through seeded Tower rooms and Delve areas (crates broken, pools waded, reflections painted and reused or refreshed, a tight planning budget) on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call, and hashes that along with the area-one sprite lookups and draws (`area1-tileset.ts`: pickups, chests and doors, loaded or not) and the Upgrades page's particle fluid (`tree-particles.ts`, over seeded frames with resizes, purchases, the shimmering rays around a node just unlocked (`unlock`, its first rank) and reduced motion) against `tests/fixtures/art-modules.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to that art.
The render golden includes decor scenes (a crate stepped on, a pool waded into, tall grass walked through) that check the effect is live before hashing, and Defend scenes (building mode, a rainy battle, the night boss wave, a stormy night zoomed in, the palette icons) stepped from the replay test's cities, which check the units and effects they exist for are present.
Lighting, weather, torch light, outside grass, and tree particles are separate modules.
Outside the entrance, `outside.ts` holds the forest clearing (`OutsideWorld`, where a forest run's hero starts on the crossroads partway up the path, `OUTSIDE_START_Y`, and whose path's foot, `atExit`, leads to the other forest; its sprite choice, fallback tile art and the tower and cave entrances; tiles are addressed by a `ForestSpot`), the crossroads' road (the row of `OUTSIDE_START_Y`) which continues to the Blacksmith's and the Hall's doors once each stands (`Roads`, `roadsOf`, on `ForestSpot` and `BoardLook`), `weather.ts` (`OutdoorWeather`) times thunder and paints sun, cloud, rain and lightning from one `WeatherFrame` of settings, and `torch-light.ts` bakes each torch's glow from a walking-distance search and a wall-aware box blur.
`tests/outdoor-art.test.ts` hashes seeded weather runs (thunder timing, sound calls, and the drawing on a recording canvas), the clearing's tiles, steps and art, and torch light fields over seeded wall maps against `tests/fixtures/outdoor-art.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to them.

**Medieval theme** (setting `medievalTheme`, "Render Medieval Theme", off by default) is ported from the TowerDefense repo: `src/medieval.css` is its `theme.css` with every selector scoped under `html.medieval` (regenerate by re-scoping, never hand-edit selectors unscoped), loaded after `style.css`, with textures in `assets/theme/`.
`ui/medieval.ts` toggles the `medieval` and `reduce-motion` root classes (at start-up and from the Settings page) and, only while the theme is on, plays `src/sound.ts`'s synthesized cues (Web Audio, no files) and `ui/flourish.ts`'s sparks on button presses; the Defend page, skill trees and `main.ts` call `play(...)`, which stays silent otherwise.
Nothing in the theme changes layout while it is off.

**Neon theme** (setting `neonTheme`, "Render Neon Theme", off by default): `src/neon.css`, every selector scoped under `html.neon` and loaded after `medieval.css`, draws the UI as warm glowing laser lines (pink, amber and yellow outlines with glow shadows on black).
`applyMedievalTheme` in `ui/medieval.ts` toggles its root class too; turning either theme on turns the other off.
In the dungeon (not the forest outside), `Renderer.draw` swaps the lit board for `drawNeonBoard`: `neon-board.ts` draws black ground, wall outlines along open sides, one neon sprite per tile kind (one for every enemy; doors show a bar per key colour), torches, and the hero, then the route and the usual feedback overlays.
Lighting, glows and decor (`decorOn`) are off while it is on, until each has a neon version.
