# Rendering

**Optional shared PNG kit.** `sprite-style.ts` enables it only with Sprites on, board sprites allowed and the Classic Game theme worn.
`BoardLook.styledSprites` passes this choice to tiles, pickups, all named enemies, four-direction heroes, torches, rising rewards and forest buildings.
The normalized terrain atlas (`sprite-terrain.ts`, generated `sprite-terrain-index.ts`) preserves every biome's texture selection and adjacency.
`spriteRevision()` participates in ground cache keys so asynchronous image arrivals repaint fallbacks.
Other Game themes keep their own drawing and portrait art; changing UI themes affects only the interface kit.

Board drawing (`Renderer`, frames, lighting, decor, popups, tile caches, the forest outside, weather), the render goldens, and the Medieval and Neon themes.

**Rendering layers.** `rendering.ts` (`Renderer`) owns the camera and smooth player interpolation: the camera, and the hero outside a run's turns, ease after their targets, while through a turn's single step inside a run (`Game.turnShown`) the hero walks at an even pace over the turn's `ms`, so it stands where that turn's shots leave from (a turn stamped after the first frame showing it walks from that frame).
The turns' shots (`volley-art.ts`): `ShotsInFlight` keeps every volley still flying (a turn's last shots land as the next's leave), and `drawVolley` starts each on the display frame nearest its tick (`shotFlight`: up to half a frame early, a tie going to the later frame), an arrow from where the hero stood as it fired, an enemy's shot homing on the hero as drawn.
An enemy an arrow felled is cleared from the board at once, but `ShotsInFlight.board` (the frame's `f.world`, one object per board) keeps it standing on its tile until that arrow lands, for every pass that reads the frame's tiles.
Each `draw()` builds one `FrameContext` (`render-frame.ts`: the camera's `BoardView`, the canvas, the board's tiles as a narrow `BoardTiles` rather than the `Game`, the `BoardLook`, and the torches, walls and glows in view).
`BoardView` (`left`, `bottom`, `n`, `s`) is the one view shape: the tile caches, decor and the forest grass all take the frame itself as their view.
`BoardLook` holds the art set (mode, height, seed, outside) and the player's settings (sprites off, reduced motion), and decides once per frame whether the board is area one (`area1`, the first Tower section's hand-drawn art and floor relief); passes read these from `f.look`, never from the game and draws the ground over it.
Outside, it then draws the tile contents, entrance, route line, hero in its grass, and weather; in the dungeon, `DungeonLight` (`dungeon-light.ts`) runs the rest of the frame in its fixed order (torch relief, decor, cast shadows, darkness and glows, tile contents, torches and lightmap, route line, hero and what stands in front of it, frame edge, vignette), calling back into a `LitBoard` the renderer builds for what it draws between the light's steps.
What the game hands the board to show of its moves (`Gain`, `Heal`, `Facing`, `Dash`, `ShownFight`, `Encounter`) is typed in `shown.ts`, so the renderer and themed boards draw them without importing `state.ts`.
Blocked-step and feedback text, then the skull over a hero struck down (`struck-down.ts`), the revival's fire and the level-up burst (`level-up.ts`), go last.
The passes live in their own modules.
`tile-painters.ts` holds the tile art (one painter per tile kind, plus the hero and torch sprites), and every painter draws onto the context it's given.
A wall tile gets a corner brick (`drawCornerBricks`) in each inner room corner only where its art runs a mortar edge for the brick to meet: the area1 sprites and the procedural walls; the other biomes' wall sprites go without, and only the area1 sprites wrap a quoin round their outer corners (`drawArea1Quoins`).
Items, the torch and the hero wear a coloured outline (`withOutline`); an enemy is baked once per name, tier, `strength` and whether it is Fast or Ranged (`bakeEnemy`, an arrow at the top right for Ranged, the Fast mark below it when both: a one-pixel black outline, then `ENEMY_LOOK`'s bands outside it and rank chevrons over it (`rankChevrons` in `rank-chevrons.ts`, `RANK_CHEVRONS`: a weak enemy's one pointing down, one for strong, two for elite, three for a boss, four for a Greater Boss), and a Fast enemy's cyan fast-forward mark at the top right, `fastMark`; the Neon enemy draws one too), and its dark-room glow in `lighting.ts` is keyed by strength too.
Only opaque bands cast torch shadows.
Ground uses the PNG tilesets (`themed-tilesets.ts`) and falls back to `drawTerrain` in `themes.ts`, the procedural art (wall faces per theme, floor contact shadows, and a small library of details) shown when sprites are off or still loading.
`tests/terrain-paint.test.ts` runs `drawTerrain` on a recording canvas over every Tower theme and several Delve areas and hashes the calls against `tests/fixtures/terrain-paint.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended art change.
`DungeonLight` (two methods: `glows(f)` starts a frame, `draw(f, board)` runs it) is the renderer's only entry point to the lighting, and owns the hand-offs between its two passes (the shadow layer, the sprite darkness, the clip regions around contents, hero and foreground): `lighting-pass.ts` (`LightingPass`) handles the Brightness darkness, object glows, torch lightmap and haze, floor relief, and vignette, and `entity-lighting.ts` (`EntityLighting`) handles torch-cast sprite shadows and directional light on sprites.
`lighting.ts` holds the lighting settings and torch flicker and sway; each torch's visibility polygon comes from `torches.ts`.
`tests/lighting-passes.test.ts` drives `DungeonLight` over seeded Tower and Delve frames (with a board that paints tile contents, the hero and assorted foregrounds) on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call (and read back pixels made up from what was drawn), runs the darkening pass alone over odd clip regions, and hashes the calls along with visibility polygons, flicker and sway against `tests/fixtures/lighting-passes.golden.json`.
Each scene numbers the canvases it makes from zero and names ones left over from earlier scenes by size, so a change to one scene doesn't shift the hashes of the others; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended lighting change.
`route-path.ts` draws the golden route line.
`shield-art.ts` (`drawShields`, first of the renderer's overlays, on every board and Game theme, above the dark) draws each standing Shielded enemy's bubble (`Board.shields`, shielded.ts): a glowing green ring of its radius with a faint fill, centred on its tile, also by the other edge in the Delve; the render scene `towerShield` captures it.
`chest-marks.ts` (`drawChestMarks`, next among the overlays, above the dark) marks each Supply and Equipment Chest in view, and the floor each leaves once opened (`Tile.chest`): a light blue or purple glow over its tile and one or two stars in its upper-left corner (`CHEST_LOOK`, whose colours `lighting-pass.ts` also gives its glow).
`boss-flash.ts` (`BossFlash`, from the renderer's overlays) pulses a golden-red glow and frame on each boss the first time it is in view in a run, so a boss floor is noticed on arrival.
`board-popups.ts` (`BoardPopups`) draws what rises off the board above everything else: each strike's damage number as it lands (a darker red on the hero, and what the shroud blocked in silver beside it; an enemy strike's thorns in yellow over the enemy as it lands, none in summary rounds, where they would only cover the drops), the HP each potion or Lifesteal heals as a blue `+N` over the hero, and each gain in turn (one second each, fading for the last quarter; a tile's contents through `paintContents`, a material's PNG from `material-sprites.ts`, an Equipment material (with its count) or piece (with its rarity's mark) as its pixel icon from `ui/equipment-icons.ts`, the amount of Gold or Silver beside its coin, or the gain's text when it has no sprite or it hasn't loaded), and `lunges` leans the hero (through the frame's `playerX`/`playerY`, so its light and shadow follow) and the enemy into their strikes.
Each popup starts the moment it happens, over any still rising (gains that came together one after another, except a kill's or a chest's drops, `Gain.drop` from `Finds`' `drops`, which start together, each its `row` of `DROP_ROW`, 0.7 tiles, from the tile: a kill's Gold over its Silver, at the tile, with Cells and Equipment's finds below; a chest's Gold at the tile, its materials below; a played-out fight's drops hold until `DROP_HOLD_MS` after its last strike lands, as its last numbers begin to fade, so they never rise through them), and they draw oldest first so newer ones sit on top, every number over every gain's sprite but the drops, so a potion's heal never hides behind the item rising from the same tile, and the drops over every number, so a kill's finds never hide behind its damage.
When the hero reaches another Tower floor, the renderer snaps it onto its new tile instead of gliding and fades a white glow behind it (the `LitBoard`'s optional `halo`, which `DungeonLight` draws after the darkness and before the hero, so it is never dimmed; the renderer gives it only while the glow lasts).
The board's fixed seeded scenes and their frame timings live in `tests/render-scenes.ts`, shared by two goldens.
Each scene's seed is its place in the job list, so a new board scene goes in `LATER_BOARD_SCENES`, after all the others, to keep every existing scene's seed.
`tests/render-calls.test.ts` (part of `npm test`, no browser needed) draws them in Node on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call, with images loaded at their real sizes, and hashes the calls at each captured frame against `tests/fixtures/render-calls.golden.json`.
It is the same on every machine, so it is the main check that a rendering refactor draws exactly the same.
`Math.pow` comes from the system C library and can differ in its last bit between Linux (CI) and Windows, so this test and the lighting golden import `tests/portable-math.ts` first, which swaps in a `pow` built from V8's own `exp` and `log`; a new golden whose hashes pass through `pow` should do the same; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to what the board draws.
`tests/render-golden.mjs` (`npm run test:render`) draws the same scenes in a browser and compares pixel hashes against `tests/fixtures/render.golden.json`.
It hashes a scene only once every image the app asked for has loaded and a capture asks for no more, fails if the scene then draws differently twice (checked for every scene when recording, otherwise only for a scene that no longer matches), and keeps every canvas on the CPU (Chrome otherwise moves a canvas there partway through a run after enough pixel reads, and the two paths blend differently), so repeated runs agree exactly.
Pixels can still differ between browsers and machines, so before changing rendering, regenerate the golden on the unchanged code with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`, then compare after your change (PNGs of the frames that changed go to `test-results/render/`, the recorded ones to `test-results/render-golden/`).
The viewport is a fixed 17×17 tiles (`VIEWPORT_TILES`; 20×20 outside); rendering density is independent of world dimensions.
`tile-cache.ts` bakes rarely-changing layers (floor, decor pixels) into one offscreen image per layer covering the view plus a margin, and repaints only when the view leaves that area, the board or tile size changes, or tile art is still loading.
Battery saver drops to 30 fps while nothing moves.
`decor-render.ts` (`DecorLayer`, the renderer's only decor entry point) budgets tile planning per frame and runs decor's passes: `decor-bake.ts` bakes each tile's static pixels for the tile cache, `decor-effects.ts` owns the live state and particle physics (splintering crates, stirred grass, spores, splashes and ripples), `decor-reflections.ts` mirrors crates, walls, tile contents, torch flames and the hero into pools (the renderer hands `drawGround` a `MirroredSprites` that paints each in tile space; the mirror lines, snapshots and reuse live in decor), and `decor-sprites.ts` paints crates, parting grass, ripple rings and drips.
Glowing blooms and fireflies scale with the Brightness setting.
Its draw passes take one `DecorFrame` (view, time, tile lookup, reduced motion).
`tests/art-modules.test.ts` walks `DecorLayer` through seeded Tower rooms and Delve areas (crates broken, pools waded, reflections painted and reused or refreshed, a tight planning budget) on a fake DOM whose canvases record every call, and hashes that along with the area-one sprite lookups and draws (`area1-tileset.ts`: pickups, chests and doors, loaded or not) and the Upgrades page's particle fluid (`tree-particles.ts`, over seeded frames with resizes, purchases, the shimmering rays around a node just unlocked (`unlock`, its first rank) and reduced motion) against `tests/fixtures/art-modules.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to that art.
The render golden includes decor scenes (a crate stepped on, a pool waded into, tall grass walked through) that check the effect is live before hashing, and Defend scenes (building mode, a rainy battle, the night boss wave, a stormy night zoomed in, the palette icons) stepped from the replay test's cities, which check the units and effects they exist for are present.
Lighting, weather, torch light, outside grass, and tree particles are separate modules.
Outside the entrance, `outside.ts` holds the forest clearing (`OutsideWorld`, where a forest run's hero starts on the crossroads partway up the path, `OUTSIDE_START_Y`, and whose path's foot, `atExit`, leads to the other forest; its sprite choice, fallback tile art and the tower and cave entrances; tiles are addressed by a `ForestSpot`), the crossroads' road (the row of `OUTSIDE_START_Y`) which continues to the Blacksmith's and the Hall's doors once each stands, and on through the trees to the left or right edge while a tier before or after the selected one is open, as the tier arrows lead (`Roads`, `roadsOf`, on `ForestSpot` and `BoardLook`; `Game.forestWorld` lays them), `weather.ts` (`OutdoorWeather`) times thunder and paints sun, cloud, rain and lightning from one `WeatherFrame` of settings, and `torch-light.ts` bakes each torch's glow from a walking-distance search and a wall-aware box blur.
`tests/outdoor-art.test.ts` hashes seeded weather runs (thunder timing, sound calls, and the drawing on a recording canvas), the clearing's tiles, steps and art, and torch light fields over seeded wall maps against `tests/fixtures/outdoor-art.golden.json`; regenerate it with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` only for an intended change to them.

**Themes** come in two kinds (`src/theme-collection.ts`, `THEMES`): a UI theme dresses the page around the board (setting `uiTheme`: `classic`, `medieval`, `neon`, `terminal`, `desktop`, `glyph`, `ember`, `yang`, `yin`, `shiro`, `kuro`, `arcade`, `system`, `orbit`, `pixel`, `doodle` or `murk`), a Game theme redraws the board itself, its tiles, hero, enemies and torches (setting `gameTheme`: `classic`, `neon`, `terminal`, `desktop`, `glyph`, `ember`, `yang`, `yin`, `shiro`, `kuro`, `arcade`, `system`, `kroz`, `orbit`, `pixel`, `doodle` or `murk`).
Classic is each kind's free look; every other theme is owned once bought (`save.themes`, `decodeThemes`): Medieval (UI) from the start (`starting`), every other for `THEME_PRICE`, 1 Gold, the one price for all in one currency (`CurrencyId`, spent through `CURRENCIES`), so moving themes to another currency is that line.
`Game.buyTheme` (`buyTheme`, `canBuyTheme`) pays it, nothing with free purchases on.
In Dev mode every theme is owned: `Game.claimThemes`, run by `main.ts`'s `update`, gives the ones not owned yet (`grantAllThemes`) and announces each with a toast (`themeToast` in `ui/theme-menu.ts`); without Dev mode it gives none.
Each theme owned adds 2.5% to all Gold banked (`themeGoldFactor`, in `RunPurse.gold` and the Gold bonuses' list).
The look worn is `uiLook` / `gameLook`: the chosen one while owned, else Classic, so an old save that chose Neon before owning it shows Classic until it does.
A new hero starts in Medieval; older saves' `medievalTheme` and `neonTheme` toggles become the two settings in `decodeSettings` (Neon having drawn both page and board), a hero who never turned Medieval on keeping the Classic page.
The Settings page's UI Themes and Game Themes buttons open each kind's menu (`ui/theme-menu.ts`, `renderThemeMenu`, drawn into the Settings page): a card per choice with its preview (`public/assets/themes/<kind>-<look>.png`, screenshots of the game in that look), name, Gold, and Equip (`Game.wearTheme`), Equipped, or Buy with the price as the currency's icon and amount (disabled while short of it); `tests/theme-collection.test.ts` covers buying, Dev mode, ownership, the Gold and the migration.

**Medieval UI theme** is ported from the TowerDefense repo: `src/medieval.css` is its `theme.css` with every selector scoped under `html.medieval` (regenerate by re-scoping, never hand-edit selectors unscoped), loaded after `style.css`, with textures in `assets/theme/`.
`ui/medieval.ts` (`applyUiTheme`) toggles the `medieval` and `reduce-motion` root classes (at start-up, from the Settings page and its theme menu) and, only while the theme is worn, plays `src/sound.ts`'s synthesized cues (Web Audio, no files) and `ui/flourish.ts`'s sparks on button presses; the Defend page, skill trees and `main.ts` call `play(...)`, which stays silent otherwise.
Nothing in the theme changes layout while it is off.
Its buttons' `position: relative` sits under `:where(html.medieval)`, and their press sinks them with `translate` and `scale` rather than `transform`, so a button the page places itself (the forest sign, absolutely positioned and shifted by a transform) keeps its place.

**Neon UI theme**: `src/neon.css`, every selector scoped under `html.neon` and loaded after `medieval.css`, draws the UI as warm glowing laser lines (pink, amber and yellow outlines with glow shadows on black).
`applyUiTheme` in `ui/medieval.ts` toggles its root class too; one UI theme is worn at a time.

**Terminal UI theme**: `src/terminal.css`, every selector scoped under `html.terminal` and loaded after `neon.css`, draws the UI as an old green-phosphor monitor shows it.
Its text is the bundled VT323 face (`assets/fonts/VT323/`, OFL, the one exception to Cinzel and only while the theme is worn; a badge's glyph keeps its system face), in one green on black, with hollow boxes, double-ruled panels, inverted selections, and every picture but the theme previews tinted green.
A fixed `body::after` lays scanlines and a dark rim over the page (never taking a touch), flickering faintly and blinking the page title's cursor unless motion is reduced.

**Desktop UI theme**: `src/desktop.css`, every selector scoped under `html.desktop` and loaded after `terminal.css`, draws the UI as an old windowed desktop computer: the app one grey window on a teal desktop, raised bevelled buttons and panels, white sunken fields and wells, navy title bars (page titles, dialog headings) with caption buttons, pale yellow tooltips for notes, and bars filling with navy blocks.
Its text is the bundled W95FA pixel face (`assets/fonts/W95FA/`, OFL, from Fontsource's `@fontsource/win95fa`; only while the theme is worn; a badge's glyph keeps its system face), in black.
Being light on a dark page, it first clears the page's own backgrounds, glows, rounded corners and text colours on every element (drawings, images, svg and the bars' `i` fills excepted) with rules lifted by `:not(#_)` over the page's class and id rules, then dresses the named windows, wells and tooltips with the same lift; a style written on an element still wins, and pseudo-elements (the red dots on what is new) are untouched.
A new page part with a dark panel needs no rule to stay readable, only one to look like a window.
**Glyph UI theme**: `src/glyph.css`, every selector scoped under `html.glyph` and loaded after `desktop.css`, draws the UI as glowing ASCII cards in many neon colours on a purple field of dim symbols (`assets/theme/glyph-field.svg`).
Its text is the bundled VT323 (as the Terminal theme's), page titles ruled like `:=Title==:`, buttons and panels framed in dashes that each glow in their own colour, turning from one to the next (cyan, orange, yellow, magenta, green, blue), and bars filled with glowing blocks.
Like Desktop it clears the page's own pane backgrounds (`:not(#_)`), but those of the panels, buttons, bars and dialogs it dresses, so the field shows through.

**Ember UI theme**: `src/ember.css`, every selector scoped under `html.ember` and loaded after `glyph.css`, draws the UI as warm cream and amber ASCII glowing on a dark maroon field of dim letters (`assets/theme/ember-field.svg`), in VT323, panels and buttons ruled in dashed and double amber lines, glowing faintly red from inside, and the HP bar a red ember.
It clears the page's own pane backgrounds as Glyph does.

**Yang and Yin UI themes**: `src/zen.css`, loaded after `ember.css`, dresses both (`html.yang`, `html.yin`, sharing their rules through `:is(.yang, .yin)` and differing only in their `--z-*` colours): Yang is ink on pale rice paper, Yin pale ink on dark stone.

**Shiro and Kuro UI themes**: `src/bare.css`, loaded after `zen.css`, dresses both (`html.shiro`, `html.kuro`, sharing their rules through `:is(.shiro, .kuro)` and differing only in their `--b-*` colours): Shiro is flat black on white, Kuro flat white on black.
Their text is the bundled Inter (`assets/fonts/Inter/`, OFL, Regular and SemiBold from Fontsource's `@fontsource/inter`; only while either is worn).
They clear every element's backgrounds, borders, corners, glows and text colours (`:not(#_)`), then give panels, tips and buttons only a flat block a shade off the page; the chosen button is filled with ink, bars are a flat track filled with ink (HP red), and red marks only what is new or dangerous.

**Arcade UI theme**: `src/arcade.css`, loaded after `bare.css`, dresses `html.arcade` as an old arcade cabinet's screen.
Its text is the bundled VT323; titles (headings, page titles) and the floor reached are in the bundled Press Start 2P (`assets/fonts/PressStart2P/`, OFL, the Latin subset from Fontsource's `@fontsource/press-start-2p`; only while it is worn), uppercase, yellow with a red drop, at 16px or 24px so its pixels land on whole pixels.
Like Shiro it first clears every element's backgrounds, borders, glows and text colours (`:not(#_)`), then frames panels and dialogs in the maze's blue double line with rounded corners, buttons in a single blue line (the chosen one lit yellow), and the board in a double outline (an outline, so it takes no room from the board); page titles carry a row of pellets under them, bars are segmented like a life meter (HP red, the rest cyan), and scanlines and a dark rim run over the whole page (`body::after`).

**System UI theme**: `src/system.css`, loaded after `arcade.css`, dresses `html.system` as an old one-bit computer: black on white with only dithered grey between (`--s-grey`, a checker of single dots, is the desktop behind everything).
Its text is the bundled Pixelify Sans (`assets/fonts/PixelifySans/`, OFL, Regular and Bold from Fontsource's `@fontsource/pixelify-sans`; only while it is worn).
Like Desktop it first clears the page's own backgrounds, glows and text colours on every element (`:not(#_)`), then draws panels as white windows in a black frame with a hard drop shadow; every page but the board's is such a window, so no text sits on the grey, while the board's page shows the desktop between its windows.
Page titles are title bars ruled with fine black lines round a white caption, with a close box at the left; dialogs have a double frame; tips are rounded help balloons; buttons are rounded push buttons that invert to white on black while pressed or chosen, as the open tab does; bars are a white track filled black (XP dithered grey).
Red marks only what is short or dangerous, and the HUD's keys keep their colour.

**Orbit UI theme**: `src/orbit.css`, loaded after `system.css`, dresses `html.orbit` as a space-age starship's console, in shapes and colours of our own rather than any show's or game's: deep space behind everything (a field of stars, `assets/theme/orbit-stars.svg`, over a faint violet and teal nebula).

Red marks only what is short or dangerous, and the HUD's keys keep their colour.

**Pixel UI theme**: `src/pixel.css`, loaded after `orbit.css`, dresses `html.pixel` as the menus of a pixel-art game of our own: a night of pixel stars (`--p-stars`, an inline SVG tile) behind chunky indigo panels.

**Doodle UI theme**: `src/doodle.css`, loaded after `pixel.css`, dresses `html.doodle` as a page drawn with a mouse in a simple paint program of no one's in particular: white paper (a spray-can speck now and then, `--d-specks`) and black text.

**Murk UI theme**: `src/murk.css`, loaded after `doodle.css`, dresses `html.murk` as Doodle after dark: dusky violet paper (a dim star or a little bat now and then, `--m-specks`) and text pale as bone.
Its text is the bundled Patrick Hand (`assets/fonts/PatrickHand/`, OFL, its one weight, Latin, from Fontsource's `@fontsource/patrick-hand`), only while it is worn.

Every UI theme that clears text colours (Medieval inks its parchment tips too) gives an enemy's inspect panel back its colours in its own tones, beside the verdict's `.safe` and `.danger`: what gets through red (`.hit-hurt`), Lifesteal's heal blue (`.hit-heal`) and the Gold drop gold (`.hit-gold`), the blue and gold usually the HUD keys' own.
What those clears would lose that carries meaning comes back in each theme's own tones: badges' symbols, the marks on the hand's cards and the level on a badge's star are left out of the text-colour clear (and the marks out of the background clear; Desktop keeps its black symbols on its grey token), the Goals line is drawn in the theme's line colour and lit in its accent up to the floor reached (completed floors lit with it, or filled with ink in Shiro, Kuro and System), and Training's next value (`.training-box.next`, also the Archives' next level) is the theme's green.
The bars' fills (`i`) keep Classic's look in those clears, and so would the locked Deck's empty slots (`.deck-reserve-slots i`) under Classic's dark shade, so the light-paged ones (Desktop, Yang and Yin, Shiro and Kuro, System, Doodle) draw those slots as faint dashed outlines in their line colour with no shade, and the Locked notice reads on the panel.

**Game themes but Classic** each draw the dungeon (not the forest outside) in their own art: a `ThemedBoard` (`themed-board.ts`; `THEMED_BOARDS` in `themed-boards.ts`, one per look) paints the ground over the whole board, then in tile space each tile's contents (a painter for each kind of contents, its table given to `contentsFrom` as `ContentPainters`, so a new tile kind doesn't compile until every board draws it), the Heart Door's heart on its own, a torch and the hero, and optionally something over it all (`over`).
In the dungeon `Renderer.draw` swaps the lit board for `drawThemedBoard`: the ground, contents (the enemy struck leaning into its lunge), torches, the route, the hero (its dash echoes too) and `over`, then the usual feedback overlays.
A Flying enemy (`Tile.flyer`) is drawn by `drawFlyers` (`flyer-art.ts`) over the board after the hero (above the dark), three tile units above its tile and at half opacity over the hero: Classic through `paintTile` with its baked enemy, which wears the wing (`wingMark`) where a Ranged enemy's arrow goes, and every Game theme (`drawThemedBoard`) through its own enemy painter with the same wing over it.
Every themed board draws an enemy's rank chevrons as Classic does (`rankChevrons`), at the tile's top left in its own colours (Ember's down the left edge, under the name's letters), beside its own marks for strength and its Ranged and Fast marks.
The boards share their rounded rectangles and the dark rim round the board (`roundRect`, `vignette`) from `themed-art.ts`.
`tests/themed-boards.test.ts` hashes every call each themed board makes for each kind of tile contents, its heart, torches, hero and ground against `tests/fixtures/themed-boards.golden.json`, the boards' only golden: the render scenes are all Classic.
`BoardLook.themed` holds the board worn (null for Classic, and always outside), so what rises off it wears that art too: a pickup's tile contents and a Heart Door's heart; coins, the Gem, materials and Equipment, with no themed art yet, keep theirs (`tests/theme-popups.test.ts`).
Lighting, glows and decor (`decorOn`) are off while one is worn, until each has a version of its own.
