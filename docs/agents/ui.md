# Pages and HUD

**PNG UI sprites.** `ui-sprites.css`, imported after the theme styles, skins buttons, panels, dialogs, fields, checkboxes, bars, banners and slots with the shared PNG kit in `public/assets/ui/frames/`.
Every rule is scoped to `html.ui-sprites`, selected by the default Medieval/Classic UI theme rather than a saved opt-in switch.
Animations and Square doors are always-visible independent settings, initially unchecked and saved immediately.
Animations controls torch flames, potion bubbles, rare key shines, stationary hero idle and death-skull frames; Reduce motion freezes these frames while other Game themes retain their own art.
The retired Sprites and Turn off board sprites controls and their saved fields are gone.
`bootstrap.ts` keeps `#app` hidden behind the initial loading screen until every deployed image, stylesheet image, interface image and font has decoded.
It imports `main.ts` only after the sprite cache is warm, fits the HUD and paints the first board before removing the loading screen, then starts the frame loop and online refreshes.
An image or font failure keeps gameplay hidden with a Retry button that reloads the page.
`sprites/assets.ts` is generated by `npm run sprites:manifest`; the production prebuild regenerates it and a test checks complete coverage of deployed art.
The art contract, reference, prompt and extraction instructions live in [UI sprites](../art/ui-sprites.md).
`sprites/style.ts` decides effective art independently for UI and Game themes: the Medieval/Classic interface uses the default kit, other UI themes override it, and only the Classic Game theme uses world sprites.
`sprites/kit.ts` provides the shared decoded PNG cache, load revisions, native PNG controls and icon pairs whose vector/text art belongs to alternate UI themes.
Its `INSPIRATION_SPRITE` and `BREAKTHROUGH_SPRITE` name the two skill-tree currencies' art (the `inspiration` and `breakthrough` `UiSprite`s in `ui/dom.ts`, the board's Inspiration popup): each borrows another sprite (the Shard, the boots) until its own exists.
`skillSprite` in `ui/dom.ts` picks each skill node's sprite, falling back on Inspiration's.
DOM icons, relic pendants, tutorial hands and Equipment actions use those themed pairs; `sprites/cards.ts` specifies distinct layered PNG card faces with live card names.
`badge-token.ts` shares PNG symbols, rarity frames and the live-number level star between the Deck, card marks, hand, details and draw reveals.
Gate signs remain live text, visible only with a loaded replacement.
`sprites/ui-details.ts` maps every Goal unlock to the shared kit and fixes the row order of the Goal/utility and ornament atlases for authoring and coverage checks.
Goal/status icons, compact HUD controls, Copy, Deck remove/check controls and Event locks use PNG pairs; the seal, forest sign and linked-card diamonds use reusable PNG components under the same theme class.
Skill nodes use PNG state frames and specific shared subjects, and Silver uses its own PNG while its original recolored art remains the alternate-theme art.
Shell icon replacement updates an existing pair rather than inserting duplicate currency or combat symbols.
`boon-icon.ts` adds theme-aware Shrine subjects to the existing text controls; the native first-choice select and all boon labels, counts, ban and ranking actions remain live.
`sprites/equipment.ts` lists Equipment's native PNG rarity/empty/selection frames and status marks, shared across its inventory, loadouts, detail and result screens through `ui-sprites.css` and `kitIcon` pairs.
`ui/control-icon.ts` maps small navigation, close, check, lock, swap and ranking symbols through `sprites/controls.ts` to native 12-pixel PNGs; the same atlas supplies the reward star and native slider parts.
Range inputs retain their original event handlers, values and keyboard behavior, with PNG track/thumb states applied only by the effective UI sprite class.
The Gold-bonus and enemy-odds layers reuse the dialog PNG, and heal/loss preview segments use palette-matched PNG fills, including Overheal.
`applyUiTheme` mirrors effective art in `ui-sprites` and `game-sprites` classes from the selected themes.
Under the kit the HUD's panel frame is 6px and its columns sit inside it (padded, with the columns re-proportioned so the actions column's framed labels fit), so the left rail's background no longer hides the frame's left edge.
The pages' currencies chevron and the run's speed arrows are bare glyphs, and a card's details show its face without a button frame round it.
The open menu's hamburger shows the kit's X (`ui-menu-close`, drawn pixel by pixel in `tools/prepare-menu-close.mjs`); other looks morph the SVG bars into an X.
The active UI kit uses the original local Tower Pixel face (`sprites/font.ts`, `assets/fonts/TowerPixel/`); text remains live, and badge glyphs keep their original typography.
PNG button edges reserve real border space: six pixels on ordinary controls and three on compact controls, with matching `border-image-width`; picture buttons keep their own component frames.
Deck card faces fill their controls without the generic button frame or an occupied hand-slot frame; empty and purchasable slots retain their inset frames, and lesson dimming, level marks and badge targeting remain separate.
The shared HUD puts full-width HP, combat stats and keys above a compact portrait beside the limited-use inventory (`.run-inventory`), with XP and the unlocked Shrine bar retained inside the portrait.
Phone action columns reserve enough width for whole play/pause labels, inventory controls wrap, ad controls stack, menu cells keep a 48-pixel minimum, and phone Training values sit above purchase controls.
`fitCombatStats` refits both combat values and HP after a font load, resize or theme change; its cache includes available width, effective classes and the font revision.

Pages see the game as a `PageGame`: its mode, run, save and whether the hero lies fallen to read (they may change a setting or a purchase in the save but never replace the save, run or mode), and the commands that change the rest; they never touch the `Renderer`.
`Game` keeps the commands of each page in desks it owns (`src/game/`), each over a `DeskHost` (`game/desk.ts`: the save, free purchases, the clock, the status line, and the hooks a command reports to: `missionDone`, `eventDone`, `researchDone`): `training` (`TrainingDesk`, which also keeps each screen's Buy Quantity), `skills` (`SkillDesk`: buying skills), `shop` (`ShopDesk`: buying Shop offers), `themes` (`ThemeDesk`: claiming, buying and wearing themes), `research` (`ResearchDesk`), `deck` (`DeckEditor`), `gear` (`GearDesk`) and `gemFinder` (`GemFinder`); beside them `RunPurse` (`game/run-purse.ts`) pays what a run finds, and `game/hero-sync.ts` keeps the runs' heroes in step with the loadout (`startingHero`, `changeLoadout`, `readyForestRuns`).
The run's own desks: `finds` (`Finds`, `game/finds.ts`) is what the run finds (kills, items, chests, doors), `activation` (`CardActivation`, `game/card-activation.ts`) is what a card does as it activates, and `fights` (`FightPlayback`, `game/fight-playback.ts`) plays and shows fights: the frame loop settles its `encounter` (`fights.finish`), and the HUD, renderer and run end dialog read it (`shownHp`, `endHp`, `shown`, `struckDown`, `revivedAt`, `defeatShownUntil`). `fights` gets `Game.take` as a constructor callback rather than through its host, so committing a step stays private to `Game`.
The game's `notices` (`NoticeDesk`, `game/notice-desk.ts`) keeps the Notices, the dots on tabs and buttons: `waiting(spot)` says whether one wears its dot (the Upgrades, Gear, Research, Deck and Shop tabs, the run's Shop, the forest's Goals button, the run's Research button, the hamburger, and inside pages the Inspiration and Breakthrough trees', Training's and Equipment's tabs), and `shown(view)` records a page or tab shown, clearing the dots it answers (`navigate` calls it for the Upgrades, Gear, Research and Deck pages, and the pages for their tabs); it is the only writer of what has been seen (`save.seen`, `treeNotices`, `equipment.seen`, `tutorials.upgrades` and `tutorials.gear`), and reads the Missions, Events, Mail and Tournament desks' own `waiting` for the hamburger (`tests/notices.test.ts`).
The Deck tab's dot also shows after a card level-up since the Deck last showed (`save.seen.cardLevels`, every card's level under its max level summed by `totalCardLevels`, set by `shown("deck")`), and `deckCount` is the number on it: the deck's cards not yet shown plus the cards with points to spend (`hasPointsToSpend` in `cards/trees.ts`), which `renderDeckCount` in `hud.ts` puts in the tab's `data-count` for the CSS to draw.
`isNew` and `met` keep the Deck page's NEW marks (`save.seen.met`, the cards met there: the starting hand from the start, and in a save from before it every card in its deck); `DeckPage.meet` calls `met` for a card's details, a move, a deck card dropped into the hand and a badge attached (`tests/card-points-lesson.test.ts`).
