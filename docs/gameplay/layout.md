# Layout

The game lays itself out for the shape of the screen.

## Portrait and landscape

On a phone held upright, or a narrow window, the HUD sits over the board and the tabs (or, inside a climb, the hand) run along the bottom.
Turn the phone on its side, or make a desktop window wide enough, and the game switches to the landscape layout at once, and back again as the screen narrows.
In landscape the board fills the screen's height on one side, with the HUD beside it.
In the forest the tabs stand upright at the far edge of the HUD's column, between the HUD and the status line; inside a climb the hand sits at the bottom of the HUD's column.
The status line and the hints that lie over the board's foot in portrait move into the HUD's column, so they never cover the board.
Pages such as Upgrades and the Shop take the board's and the HUD's place, the tabs a column of their own beside them, at the same edge as in the forest.

**Landscape layout on wide screens**, under Settings, is on to begin with; turned off, the game keeps the portrait layout whatever the screen's shape.

## Handedness

**Handedness**, under Settings, is Right to begin with: the controls gather at the right, under the right thumb.
Left mirrors the layout: in the HUD the menu, Shop and play button stand at its left edge, and the menu opens rightward from it, in portrait and landscape alike.
In landscape the board moves to the right, and the tabs or the hand to the left.

## Battery saver

**Battery saver**, under Settings, is off to begin with.
On, the game turns black and white so a phone's screen lights as little as it can, and draws far fewer frames.
The board becomes simple white pixel art on pure black, in the forest and inside a climb alike: walls are a thin line where they meet open ground, the forest's edge a row of pine trees, open ground a faint dot, and everything on it a small white sprite.
Enemies grow, and gain horns, spikes, a skull or a crown, with their strength, and still wear their rank chevrons; small marks show a Ranged enemy (an arrow at the top right), a Fast one (chevrons at the bottom right), an Armored one (a shield at the right), an Aggroed one (an exclamation mark at the bottom left) and a stunned one.
With colour gone, keys and the locks they open share a shape: gold keys have a round bow, blue a square one and red a diamond, and each door shows the shape of every key it takes; a Steel Door shows rivets, a Wooden Door planks and a Heart Door a heart.
A percent potion's liquid is a checker; a Supply Chest wears one star and an Equipment Chest two; a lit torch is a small flame in its tile's corner, a spent one grey.
Damage numbers, rising rewards, marks and the other feedback still show, in grey.
Damage Visual's numbers on enemies and items are drawn in small pixel digits on a black box at the tile's lower left, so they read at this size: a fight that would defeat you shows black digits on a white box, and a certain Instakill's 0 is grey.
The hand's cards wear white pixel icons of their own, each matching what it goes for.
The page around the board is white on black, without animations, glows or colours, and its pictures are grey.
The board draws about fifteen frames a second while something on it moves and two or three while all is still, instead of the screen's full rate; the hero steps from tile to tile rather than gliding, and the weather and decor are off.
Battery saver wears its own look over the UI and Game themes chosen, which come back as it is turned off.
It also gathers the page into one small centred column: the room shrinks into a white square in the middle of the screen.
Turned on its side (with Landscape layout on wide screens on), the square stands at the left as tall as the screen allows, and the row of stats, the hand and the Training bar gather in a column beside it (at the left for the left hand).
The top bar is gone; above the square a short centred row shows HP, ATK, DEF, the keys held, the floor, then the Gold, Silver and Gems held, each a small white icon and its number, with Back, then Play or Pause and Settings beside them (Back, Enter and Settings in the forest), and the Gem button, with its Gems, while they can be claimed.
The hand sits just under the square, its cards smaller and their levels smaller still, and the card at work is underlined; the hand's row is as tall as an open Training group's, so the buttons below stay put.
A hand too wide for the column scrolls sideways from its first card.
There is no text under the board.
Back turns Battery saver off and brings the normal page back; Settings only opens the Settings page, where Battery saver can also be turned off.
While no card can act, a red warning sign stands first in the row of buttons; pressing it ends the climb, as End Climb does.
**Battery saver white**, just under the Battery saver setting while it is on, dims every white on the screen, from full down to 5%, where white is barely lighter than the black.
A tap anywhere brings the white up to full quickly; it stays full while the taps keep coming, and a few seconds after the last one fades back to the brightness chosen.

The **Battery Saver** UI theme and Game theme, sold in the Event Store like the other themes, wear battery saver's black and white look (its board, its card icons and its white-on-black page) at the normal size and frame rate, with the usual top bar.
