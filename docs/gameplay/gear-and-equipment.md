# Gear and Equipment

## Provisions

**Provisions** are limited-use items for a run, bought with Gold on the **Gear** tab and kept for good: each one owned is a use that every run carries, a run already inside included.
None is sold from the start: each opens with its own skill, 5 Inspiration each in the Inspiration tree for the three below (the **Traveler's Elixir** skill after Regen, **Vanguard Banner** after ATK Up, under it, and **Aegis Charm** after DEF Up, under it), and **Bombs** after Critical Prediction, left of it; and in the Breakthrough tree for the Gold Key.
The Gear tab, and its Provisions tab, show only once one is open; the Gear tab shows too once Equipment or [Arcana](arcana.md) has opened.
The first time one opens, a note says to press the Gear tab to buy provisions, and a hand points at the tab, on the forest's board and on the Inspiration and Breakthrough trees, until it is pressed.
None of them adds anything as a run starts: inside a run, each shows as a button beside Ignore and Target with its uses left, and a press uses one at once (it is no turn of its own).
A **Traveler's elixir** fills HP to max HP, the HP bar filling up to it; with HP at max (or above it), it does nothing and isn't spent.
A **Vanguard Banner** raises ATK by 20% for the next 100 turns, and an **Aegis charm** raises DEF by 20% for the next 100 turns; while one is on, the hero glows red (the Vanguard Banner) or blue (the Aegis charm), its button is lit, and another of the same kind can't be used until it runs out.
A boost adds 20% of the stat as it was when pressed, and takes that much back off when it runs out.
Undo never gives a use back and never takes an effect away: a heal stays healed, and a boost keeps counting down, as if no turn had been undone.
Uses come back only with the next run.
The tab has a **?** at the top that explains provisions (your Gold shows in the currencies bar above); each provision's icon carries the number owned in a circle at its bottom right, which bounces as you buy one, and its **Buy** button stands at the row's right.
Every provision costs 100 Gold, and each one after a hundred times the last (10,000, 1,000,000, 100,000,000 …).
Provisions bought before they became uses (when they gave starting stats) were reset to none.
Once **Extra Key** (1 Breakthrough, after Movement Speed in the Breakthrough tree) is owned, Provisions also sell a **Gold Key**, which uses itself: each owned gives a run one gold key on the floor it starts on, and again on the first floor 100 or more floors past the last that gave them (floor 1, then 101, 201 …; a floor Skip climbs past still counts, so the next floor gives them).
In the Delve, floors are its equivalent floors.
The keys come as a floor begins, so undo can't take them back.
Once the Inspiration skill **Bombs** (5 Inspiration, left of Critical Prediction) is owned, Provisions also sell **Bombs**: each one owned is a Bomb every run carries.
Its button shows the Bombs left: press it, then tap an enemy, and the Bomb explodes on its tile, dealing that enemy your ATK, critical and Super Critical hits rolled as for a strike (its DEF doesn't reduce it).
An enemy the Bomb fells is cleared and pays out as if you had beaten it; a tap on a tile with no enemy uses no Bomb, and pressing the button again puts the Bomb away.
Unlike the other provisions, throwing a Bomb is a turn of its own, so undo takes it back, Bomb included, without paying the kill twice.
Bombs used come back only with the next run.

## Equipment

**Equipment** opens by claiming Tower II's floor 60 checkpoint on the Goals screen, whose dialog offers to visit the Blacksmith.
A **Blacksmith** then stands in the forest, up and left of the path; tapping it (or the Gear tab, which opens with Equipment even with no provision open) opens the Equipment screen: the hero's starting slots in a plus-shaped grid (the Tower/Delve switch at its top left) over a grid of every piece owned, with List, Assemble, Craft and Acquire along the bottom, and how it all works behind the ? beside the loadout's heading.
Initially, only Helmet (top), Shield (left), Chestplate (center), Sword (right) and Boots (bottom) are open.
Other categories have no unlock schedule yet; existing owned categories remain open.
The hero wears one piece in each open category; the inventory (up to 500 pieces) is shared, and the Tower's and the Delve's heroes each wear their own loadout.
Each piece has its own pixel icon, so pieces of one category tell apart at a glance.
Pieces come in Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic and Ancestral, shown by word, letter (C, U, R, E, L, M, A) and colour, and an Ancestral piece can carry up to five Stars (*Ancestral ★3*).
With Settings → **Sprites** on, Common, Uncommon and Rare have matching pixel-art PNG frames (Epic and above wear the Rare frame in their own colour for now), empty slots have recessed frames, selected pieces have brass corner marks, and worn and Unique pieces have PNG symbols.
Names, rarity letters and levels remain readable text; Sprites defaults to off and non-default UI themes supply their own look.
Each starting category has three Standard pieces, from Equipment Chests, the Craft view or Gem pulls, and three Uniques, from Gem pulls only.
A Gem pull (20 Gems, or 200 for ten, in one category; or 10% less, 18 and 180, for a piece of any unlocked category) is 60% Common, 29% Uncommon and 8% Rare, each a Standard piece, and 2.5% Epic and 0.5% Legendary, each a Unique; the 120th pull in a row without an Epic or better is an Epic Unique, each category and pulls of all types counting their own.
Opening Equipment gives you 10 **Equipment Tickets**: each pays for one pull in place of its Gems, and pulls use your tickets first, then Gems for the rest (the buttons show both).
One of your first 10 ticket pulls is sure to bring an Epic Unique.
The first time you open Equipment, a welcome explains tickets, the rates and pity.
**Rates**, beside the buttons, shows each piece's chance; once you own a five-Star piece it lets you leave Uniques out of future pulls (a piece's first five-Star copy leaves it out at once), and pulls whose every Unique is left out can't be bought until you turn some back on.
There are four equipment resources: Quilted Padding for Helmets, Iron Rivets for Shields, Whetstones for Swords, and Hobnails for Boots.
Crafting a Common Standard costs 25 of each resource in its recipe, without Gold or Gems.
Chestplates require all four resources.
The future corner pieces each combine their two neighbours' resources: an Amulet between the Shield and the Helmet uses Rivets and Padding.
A piece starts at level 0, and leveling raises only its **main effect**: Swords ATK, Shields DEF, Helmets Arcanum Damage and Boots Gold found, each a percent that starts at its rarity's base (+1% ATK at Common, +30% at Ancestral) and grows with every level.
A Chestplate raises max HP the same way, and its efficiency also raises the other worn pieces' main effects by a smaller percent; each Star multiplies a piece's main effect by 1.04 more (×1.20 at five).
Leveling, Refine and Improve require every input in the same recipe; leveling also costs Gold (2,000 for level 1, rising to quintillions near 300) and stops at the rarity's cap: Common 20, Uncommon 40, Rare 60, Epic 80, Legendary 120, Mythic 160, Ancestral 200, and 20 more for each Star, up to 300.
Supply Chests and mission rewards pay only the four base resources, and Equipment Chests and Gem pulls only produce unlocked categories.
Old material balances convert into the corresponding base resources without losing units.
A boss or Greater Boss has a 1.5% chance to drop upgrade materials and a 0.5% chance to drop a Standard piece; other kills drop neither (without an enemy card's tree). With the Chest card in the hand, a treasure chest may be a Supply Chest (upgrade materials) or an Equipment Chest (a Standard piece, up to Rare); see [The hand and cards](hand-and-cards.md#card-bonuses).
Pieces assemble into the next rarity, keeping the level of the one you choose: three copies of an Uncommon or Rare piece; two copies of an Epic or Legendary piece and one more piece of its category at that rarity (a Standard piece serves, and is chosen first); two Mythic copies make an Ancestral; and an Ancestral piece with a Legendary copy of itself gains a Star.
Common pieces never merge (wear them as they are or salvage them), and Standard pieces go no higher than Legendary.
Assemble shows the recipe in words with the pieces going in, numbered, beside the result; pressing Assemble melts them into glowing pixel slag that flows together and rises into the new piece, then *Assemble Complete!*.
**Auto-assemble**, beside *Choose the piece to keep*, lists every rarity merge your pieces make at once (never a Star), each checked: worn pieces take no part, a locked piece is only ever the one kept, and each merge keeps the highest-level piece and uses up the lowest-level copies and the cheapest third piece.
Uncheck any to leave them out, then Assemble makes the rest at once, without the melt; the ✕ or a press outside the list cancels.
Dismantling breaks unwanted pieces into their recipe's first base resource, never returning what leveling cost.
Locked and worn pieces are never dismantled or merged away.
Each piece also has **substats**, in slots its level opens: at levels 1, 41, 61, 81, 121, 161, 201 and 241 (a Standard piece reaches four, a Unique all eight), each holding a substat from its category's pool, Uncommon to Ancestral but never rarer than the piece: Swords roll offense (ATK %, crits, Super Crits, Piercing, Armor Break, Combo ATK and the Archer's shots), Shields defense (DEF, shroud, max HP and Regen %, Thorns, Parry, Lifesteal, healing and the Aether Shield), Helmets arcana (Arcanum Damage and every attribute of every arcanum), Boots utility (Gold and Silver, Interest, Key Efficiency by colour, Free Upgrade and Enemy Level Skip chances) and Chestplates efficiency (XP, speed, trainers, research, drops, chests, Offline Gold, Dash and End Rush tiles).
Every substat is a percent, points of a chance or a whole count, never a flat stat; a few discrete ones (extra bolts, ghosts, Rebounds, beams, Multishot Targets, red Key Efficiency) roll only from a higher rarity.
A substat doesn't grow with the piece's level; a rarer one is stronger (Rare ×1.5 an Uncommon's).
A new slot's first roll is free (choose one of three); after that, **Refine** pays for three new candidates beside the current substat, which stays unless you take one, and every Refine counts toward that slot's Refinement: every fifth guarantees a top-rarity candidate and every tenth earns a Choice of any substat outright.
Merging keeps a piece's substats and Refinement.
Each Unique also has a **power** of its own, stronger at each rarity from Epic to Ancestral (Stars raise its main effect, not its power); its piece's page shows it, and the loadout's summary lists those worn:
- Raptor Blade: more Super Crit chance, and a Super Crit makes the next 5 strikes Super Crits, carried from fight to fight.
- Kinetic Mallet: a Dash Combo lasts more turns without a fight.
- Soul Reaper: some enemies (never bosses) are marked for death with a red X in their tile's corner, the same ones however often you retry: your first strike or arrow destroys them.
- Zenith Guard: Regen heals on past max HP, part of the way to the Overheal ceiling.
- Cradle of Life: a stronger Aether Shield that regains more.
- Amber Prison: each hit that doesn't kill weakens its enemy's blows and slows its strikes, shots and steps, up to half.
- Equilibrium Tether: ATK, DEF, max HP and Regen each count a quarter of their ranks together, and every rank trained in a climb, on any row, adds to all four.
- Mobius Cipher: every potion, chest, shard and key you pick up makes the next of its kind yield more for the rest of the run.
- Mirror Vault: damage you take is stored as Retribution, multiplied, and struck back after your next fight's first strike or your next arrow.
- Lich King's Crown: ghosts rush every boss as it comes in shot range.
- Psionic Tempest: Ricochet Shot sometimes strikes the enemy its arrow hit too.
- Challenger's Defiance: Overseer's beam stays on a boss, and while a boss stands on the floor you strike the other enemies far harder.
- Chrono Strider: arcana cool down faster, each potion takes turns off their cooldowns, and Aurum Bloom finds more Golden Floors.
- Jackpot Spurs: more Gold for each Free Upgrade that came up on the floor.
- Hoarder Greaves: more ATK the more keys you hold.
