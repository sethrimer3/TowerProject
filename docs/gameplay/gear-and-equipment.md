# Gear and Equipment

## Provisions

**Provisions** are limited-use items for a run, bought with Gold on the **Gear** tab and kept for good: each one owned is a use that every run carries, a run already inside included.
None is sold from the start: each opens with its own skill, 5 Inspiration each in the Inspiration tree for the three below (the **Traveler's Elixir** skill after Regen, **Vanguard Banner** after ATK Up, left of it, and **Aegis Charm** after DEF Up, left of it), and in the Breakthrough tree for the Gold Key and Bombs.
The Gear tab, and its Provisions tab, show only once one is open.
The first time one opens, a note says to press the Gear tab to buy provisions, and a hand points at the tab, on the forest's board and on the Inspiration and Breakthrough trees, until it is pressed.
None of them adds anything as a run starts: inside a run, each shows as a button beside Ignore and Target with its uses left, and a press uses one at once (it is no turn of its own).
A **Traveler's elixir** fills HP to max HP, the HP bar filling up to it; with HP at max (or above it), it does nothing and isn't spent.
A **Vanguard Banner** raises ATK by 20% for the next 100 turns, and an **Aegis charm** raises DEF by 20% for the next 100 turns; while one is on, the hero glows red (the Vanguard Banner) or blue (the Aegis charm), its button is lit, and another of the same kind can't be used until it runs out.
A boost adds 20% of the stat as it was when pressed, and takes that much back off when it runs out.
Undo never gives a use back and never takes an effect away: a heal stays healed, and a boost keeps counting down, as if no turn had been undone.
Uses come back only with the next run.
The tab has a **?** at the top that explains provisions (your Gold shows in the currencies bar above); each provision's icon carries the number owned in a circle at its bottom right, which bounces as you buy one, and its **Buy** button stands at the row's right.
Every provision costs 100 Gold, and each one after a hundred times the last (10,000, 1,000,000, 100,000,000 …).
Provisions bought before they became uses (when they gave starting stats) were reset to none.
Once **Extra Key** (1 Breakthrough, after Movement Speed in the Breakthrough tree) is owned, Provisions also sell a **Gold Key**, which uses itself: each owned gives a run one gold key on the floor it starts on, and again on the first floor 100 or more floors past the last that gave them (floor 1, then 101, 201 …; a floor Skip climbs past still counts, so the next floor gives them).
In the Delve, floors are its equivalent floors.
The keys come as a floor begins, so undo can't take them back.
Once the Breakthrough skill **Bombs** (5 Breakthrough, below Movement Speed) is owned, Provisions also sell **Bombs**: each one owned is a Bomb every run carries.
Its button shows the Bombs left: press it, then tap an enemy, and the Bomb explodes on its tile, dealing that enemy your ATK (its DEF doesn't reduce it).
An enemy the Bomb fells is cleared and pays out as if you had beaten it; a tap on a tile with no enemy uses no Bomb, and pressing the button again puts the Bomb away.
Unlike the other provisions, throwing a Bomb is a turn of its own, so undo takes it back, Bomb included, without paying the kill twice.
Bombs used come back only with the next run.

## Equipment

**Equipment** opens by claiming Tower II's floor 60 checkpoint on the Goals screen, whose dialog offers to visit the Blacksmith.
A **Blacksmith** then stands in the forest, up and left of the path; tapping it (or the Gear tab, which opens with Equipment even with no provision open) opens the Equipment screen: the hero's nine slots in a 3×3 grid shaped like the hero (the Tower/Delve switch at its top left) over a grid of every piece owned, with List, Assemble and Acquire along the bottom, and how it all works behind the ? beside the loadout's heading.
The hero wears one piece in each of nine categories (Weapon, Chestplate, Helmet, Gloves, Boots, Cape, Belt, Ring, Amulet); the inventory (up to 500 pieces) is shared, and the Tower's and the Delve's heroes each wear their own loadout.
Each Unique piece (and the Silver Band) has its own pixel icon, so pieces of one category tell apart at a glance.
Pieces come in Common, Uncommon and Rare, shown by word, letter and colour.
With Settings → **Sprites** on, the three rarities have matching pixel-art PNG frames, empty slots have recessed frames, selected pieces have brass corner marks, and worn and Unique pieces have PNG symbols.
Names, rarity letters and levels remain readable text; Sprites defaults to off and non-default UI themes supply their own look.
Standard pieces come only from Equipment Chests (below); the three Unique pieces of each category come only from Gem pulls (20 Gems, or 200 for ten, in one category; or 10% less, 18 and 180, for a piece of any category; 72% Common, 25% Uncommon, 3% Rare, and the 100th pull in a row without a Rare in a category is a Rare).
Upgrade materials (one category's) level a piece with Gold up to its rarity's cap (20, 40, 60).
Kills drop neither pieces nor materials: with the Chest card in the hand, a treasure chest may be a Supply Chest (upgrade materials) or an Equipment Chest (a Standard piece, up to Rare); see [The hand and cards](hand-and-cards.md#card-bonuses).
Three copies of a piece at Uncommon or above assemble into the next rarity (Common pieces never merge: wear them as they are or salvage them), keeping the level of the one you choose (Assemble shows *3× → 1×* with the three pieces going in, numbered, beside the result; pressing Assemble melts them into glowing pixel slag that flows together and rises into the new piece, then *Assemble Complete!*).
**Auto-assemble**, beside *Choose the piece to keep*, lists every merge your pieces make at once, each checked: worn pieces take no part, a locked piece is only ever the one kept, and each merge keeps the highest-level piece and uses up the lowest-level copies.
Uncheck any to leave them out, then Assemble makes the rest at once, without the melt; the ✕ or a press outside the list cancels.
Dismantling breaks unwanted pieces into their material, never returning what leveling cost.
Locked and worn pieces are never dismantled or merged away.
Besides its fixed lines, each piece has **effect slots**: one at Common, a second from level 21 (so Uncommon and above) and a third from level 41 (Rare), each holding an effect from its category's pool, never rarer than the piece.
A new slot's first roll is free (choose one of three); after that, **Refine** pays for three new candidates beside the current effect, which stays unless you take one, and every Refine counts toward that slot's Refinement: every fifth guarantees a top-rarity candidate and every tenth earns a Choice of any effect outright.
Merging keeps a piece's effects and Refinement.
