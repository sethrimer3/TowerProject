# Crafting, Materials, Enemy Drops, and Equipment

**Status:** Design source of truth for the planned crafting/equipment system. Gameplay implementation is intentionally separate.

This document defines the first-pass economy for persistent crafting resources, enemy-specific materials, treasure chests, craftable equipment, enhancement materials, equipment inventory, and salvage.

## 1. Core design rules

1. **Generated treasure chests no longer contain artifacts/heirlooms and never directly upgrade equipped gear.**
2. A generated treasure chest always awards **Gold** and may additionally award crafting materials.
3. The two new persistent crafting-resource families are:
   - **Metal Bars** — the primary material for constructing equipment.
   - **Gems** — rarer materials used to enhance equipment during crafting.
4. **Monster parts are materials, not currencies.** Every enemy species has one common part and one rare part.
5. Bars + common monster parts make the base item. Gems + rare monster parts provide optional enhancements.
6. Crafted equipment, crafting materials, and the equipment inventory are **persistent across runs** and usable in both Tower and Delve.
7. Tower floor and Delve depth use the same progression scale for loot unlocks:
   - `equivalentFloor = towerFloor` in Tower.
   - `equivalentFloor = floor(delveDepth / 10)` in Delve.
   - Therefore Tower floor 70 and Delve depth 700 have the same material unlock tier.
8. Existing Tower **clear-reward chests** (Silver/Gold/Platinum clear rewards that grant Inspiration) are a separate reward system. This document changes generated `treasure` chests, not clear-reward chests.

## 2. Current enemy roster

These are the concrete enemy species currently generated by the game. The generic stat archetypes in `config.ts` are not separate enemy species and therefore do not receive their own drop tables.

| Enemy | Current Tower range | Current Delve range | Common unique drop | Base common chance | Rare unique drop | Base rare chance |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| **Cinder slime** | Floors 0–4 | Depth 0–34 | Cinder Slime Blob | 35% | Ember Nucleus | 2.0% |
| **Bone sentinel** | Floors 5–9 | Depth 35–69 | Sentinel Bone | 35% | Gilded Marrow | 2.0% |
| **Dusk wing** | Floors 10–14 | Depth 70–104 | Dusk Feather | 35% | Eclipse Pinion | 2.0% |
| **Ash warden** | Floor 15+ | Depth 105+ | Ashen Plate Shard | 35% | Warden Sigil | 2.0% |

### Enemy-drop rules

- Common and rare rolls are **independent**. One kill can theoretically drop both.
- Common success awards **1–3** of that enemy's common material.
- Rare success awards **1** rare material.
- These are **base chances** before any future loot-chance research, equipment modifiers, elite modifiers, or events.
- Materials are credited immediately to persistent crafting inventory when the enemy dies.
- Enemy materials are never lost on player death.

### Rare-part enhancement identities

Rare monster parts are intended to provide **flat or direct reinforcement bonuses**, while gems are primarily percentage-based enhancements.

| Rare material | Enhancement per unit added while crafting |
|---|---|
| Ember Nucleus | `+1` flat Attack |
| Gilded Marrow | `+1` flat Defense |
| Eclipse Pinion | `+3` Max HP |
| Warden Sigil | `+1` flat Attack and `+1` flat Defense |

This deliberately supports recipes like adding 7 Gilded Marrow to an item for `+7 Defense`.

## 3. Metal bars

A treasure chest has a **28% base chance** to contain one stack of metal bars. If the bar roll succeeds, it drops `2–5` bars, plus `+1` to both the minimum and maximum stack size for every 40 equivalent floors reached.

Example: at equivalent floor 80, the stack range is `4–7` bars.

### Metal progression

| Metal bar | First appears | Relative equipment tier |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Iron Bar** | Floor 0 / Depth 0 | 1 |
| **Steel Bar** | Floor 15 / Depth 150 | 2 |
| **Silversteel Bar** | Floor 30 / Depth 300 | 3 |
| **Embersteel Bar** | Floor 50 / Depth 500 | 4 |
| **Starsteel Bar** | Floor 75 / Depth 750 | 5 |
| **Voidsteel Bar** | Floor 100 / Depth 1000 | 6 |

### Metal selection after the 28% bar roll succeeds

| Equivalent floor | Iron | Steel | Silversteel | Embersteel | Starsteel | Voidsteel |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–14 | 100% | — | — | — | — | — |
| 15–29 | 70% | 30% | — | — | — | — |
| 30–49 | 35% | 45% | 20% | — | — | — |
| 50–74 | 15% | 25% | 40% | 20% | — | — |
| 75–99 | — | 10% | 20% | 40% | 30% | — |
| 100+ | — | — | 10% | 20% | 40% | 30% |

The intent is that climbing higher makes precious bars progressively more common instead of merely adding them to an ever-growing flat loot pool.

## 4. Gems

Gems are rare optional enhancement materials. Each gem has its own independent **base per-chest roll**. If more than one gem roll succeeds, the chest may contain more than one gem type; this should remain uncommon.

Each successful gem roll awards **1 gem** unless later research modifies stack size.

| Gem | First appears | Base chance formula per generated chest | Crafting enhancement per gem |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Garnet** | Floor 0 / Depth 0 | `min(4.0%, 1.5% + 0.05% × E)` | `+0.5% Attack` |
| **Sapphire** | Floor 20 / Depth 200 | `min(3.0%, 0.5% + 0.05% × (E - 20))` | `+0.5% Defense` |
| **Emerald** | Floor 40 / Depth 400 | `min(2.5%, 0.3% + 0.05% × (E - 40))` | `+0.5% Max HP` |
| **Ruby** | Floor 55 / Depth 550 | `min(2.0%, 0.2% + 0.05% × (E - 55))` | `+1.0% Attack` |
| **Amethyst** | Floor 70 / Depth 700 | `min(1.5%, 0.1% + 0.10% × (E - 70))` | `+1.0% Defense` |
| **Diamond** | Floor 90 / Depth 900 | `min(1.0%, 0.05% + 0.05% × (E - 90))` | `+1.0% Max HP` |

`E` is the equivalent floor. A gem has a 0% chance before its listed unlock.

### Example: Amethyst ramp

| Tower floor | Delve depth equivalent | Amethyst chance per chest |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 69 | 690 | 0% |
| 70 | 700 | 0.10% |
| 71 | 710 | 0.20% |
| 72 | 720 | 0.30% |
| 75 | 750 | 0.60% |
| 80 | 800 | 1.10% |
| 84+ | 840+ | 1.50% cap |

This is the intended model for newly unlocked precious materials: they first appear extremely rarely, then become more attainable as the player pushes farther.

## 5. Generated treasure chest contents

A generated `treasure` chest uses the following loot sequence.

### Guaranteed drop

**Gold: 100% chance**

Gold amount:

`3 + floor(E / 5) + randomInt(0, 3 + floor(E / 10))`

Examples:

| Equivalent floor | Gold range |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 3–6 |
| 20 | 7–12 |
| 50 | 13–21 |
| 70 | 17–27 |
| 100 | 23–36 |

### Additional independent material rolls

| Chest component | Chance | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| Metal bar stack | 28% | One metal stack selected from the progression table above |
| Empty Vial | 4% | 1–2 Empty Vials |
| Gem rolls | Varies by gem and progression | Each gem uses its own table/formula above |

### Explicit exclusions

Generated treasure chests **do not** directly contain:

- Artifacts
- Heirlooms
- Finished equipment
- Direct permanent Attack/Defense upgrades
- Automatic gear-quality upgrades

Those behaviors should be removed from the current `treasure` collection path when this system is implemented.

### Utility crafting material: Empty Vial

Empty Vials are the initial non-equipment-focused chest material and exist to support consumable recipes. They intentionally remain a minority of chest material drops so that most chest materials feed the equipment loop.

Initial example recipe:

- **Cinder Tonic** — `1 Empty Vial + 3 Cinder Slime Blobs`
- One-time use.
- Restore `35 HP`, without increasing Max HP.

Future enemies can support equivalent recipes such as `Empty Vial + Red Slime material -> Red Potion` without changing the inventory architecture.

## 6. Equipment slots

The player has one equipped item in each of these slots:

1. **Weapon** — initial weapon family: Sword
2. **Shield**
3. **Helmet**
4. **Chestplate**
5. **Leggings**
6. **Boots**
7. **Gloves**
8. **Necklace**
9. **Ring**

Crafted equipment persists across runs. Equipped items contribute their stats to the player's starting/base stats in both Tower and Delve.

The Gear page should contain three internal views:

- **Equipped** — the nine equipment slots.
- **Inventory** — all crafted but unequipped equipment.
- **Crafting** — recipes, material selection, optional gem/rare-part enhancement, and craft confirmation.

## 7. Base craft recipes

Every equipment piece uses:

- the selected metal tier's bar type, and
- a specific common monster material appropriate to the item.

The required quantities do **not** increase for more precious metals. Higher-tier bars are already rarer and therefore serve as the progression gate.

| Slot | Base bars | Required common monster material | Common amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Sword | 40 | Cinder Slime Blob | 12 |
| Shield | 50 | Sentinel Bone | 15 |
| Helmet | 30 | Sentinel Bone | 8 |
| Chestplate | 70 | Ashen Plate Shard | 20 |
| Leggings | 55 | Ashen Plate Shard | 15 |
| Boots | 25 | Dusk Feather | 8 |
| Gloves | 20 | Cinder Slime Blob | 6 |
| Necklace | 15 | Dusk Feather | 5 |
| Ring | 10 | Sentinel Bone | 4 |

Example base recipe:

- **Iron Shield** = `50 Iron Bars + 15 Sentinel Bones`
- **Embersteel Shield** = `50 Embersteel Bars + 15 Sentinel Bones`

## 8. Craftable equipment sets and base stats

Every metal tier can craft all nine slots. The names are deliberately predictable so the UI can generate them without a bespoke item-name table.

### Metal set tiers

| Tier | Set prefix | Power multiplier |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Iron | 1.0 |
| 2 | Steel | 1.6 |
| 3 | Silversteel | 2.4 |
| 4 | Embersteel | 3.5 |
| 5 | Starsteel | 5.0 |
| 6 | Voidsteel | 7.0 |

### Slot stat formulas

Let `P` be the metal tier's power multiplier. Round all results to the nearest whole number, with a minimum of 1 for any listed non-zero stat.

| Craftable item | Base stats before enhancements |
|---|---|
| `[Metal] Sword` | `Attack = round(4 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Shield` | `Defense = round(3 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Helmet` | `Defense = round(1.5 × P)`, `Max HP = round(5 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Chestplate` | `Defense = round(3 × P)`, `Max HP = round(10 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Leggings` | `Defense = round(2 × P)`, `Max HP = round(7 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Boots` | `Defense = round(1 × P)`, `Max HP = round(4 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Gloves` | `Attack = round(1.5 × P)`, `Defense = round(0.5 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Necklace` | `Attack = round(0.5 × P)`, `Max HP = round(8 × P)` |
| `[Metal] Ring` | `Attack = round(1 × P)`, `Defense = round(0.5 × P)` |

This produces the following unenhanced examples:

| Item | Attack | Defense | Max HP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Iron Sword | 4 | — | — |
| Iron Shield | — | 3 | — |
| Iron Chestplate | — | 3 | 10 |
| Embersteel Sword | 14 | — | — |
| Embersteel Shield | — | 11 | — |
| Embersteel Chestplate | — | 11 | 35 |
| Voidsteel Sword | 28 | — | — |
| Voidsteel Shield | — | 21 | — |
| Voidsteel Chestplate | — | 21 | 70 |

## 9. Optional crafting enhancements

Enhancements are chosen **at craft time**. They are consumed along with the base materials and become permanently attached to that specific crafted item.

An item may use:

- up to **10 total gems**, and
- up to **10 total rare monster parts**.

Different gem/rare types may be mixed within those budgets.

### Enhancement resolution

1. Calculate the item's base metal stats.
2. Add all **flat rare-part bonuses**.
3. Sum all applicable **percentage gem bonuses**.
4. Apply percentage bonuses to the item's resulting stat contribution.
5. Store the finalized bonuses on the crafted item so later balance-table changes do not silently mutate already-crafted gear unless a migration intentionally does so.

### Example enhanced shield

Base:

- **Iron Shield** = `50 Iron Bars + 15 Sentinel Bones`
- Base Defense: `3`

Optional enhancement:

- `5 Rubies` = `+5% Attack`
- `7 Gilded Marrow` = `+7 flat Defense`

Resulting crafted item contributes:

- `Defense: 10`
- `Attack: +5%`

The percentage Attack modifier is valid even on a shield with no base Attack; it modifies the player's total applicable Attack while the shield is equipped.

## 10. Equipment inventory and equipping

Each crafted item is an individual persistent inventory object rather than a count of a generic item.

Minimum stored fields:

```ts
type EquipmentSlot =
  | "weapon"
  | "shield"
  | "helmet"
  | "chestplate"
  | "leggings"
  | "boots"
  | "gloves"
  | "necklace"
  | "ring";

type CraftedEquipment = {
  id: string;
  slot: EquipmentSlot;
  name: string;
  metal: MetalId;
  baseRecipe: MaterialStack[];
  enhancements: MaterialStack[];
  flatAttack: number;
  flatDefense: number;
  flatMaxHp: number;
  percentAttack: number;
  percentDefense: number;
  percentMaxHp: number;
};
```

Recommended persistent save structure:

```ts
materials: Record<MaterialId, number>;
equipmentInventory: CraftedEquipment[];
equipped: Partial<Record<EquipmentSlot, string>>; // equipment item id
```

Equipping a new item in an occupied slot automatically unequips the previous item back into inventory.

## 11. Salvage / destroying obsolete equipment

The Inventory view must provide a deliberate **Salvage** action for obsolete crafted equipment.

### Base salvage, before research

Return:

- **40% of the metal bars** used in the base recipe, rounded down.
- **40% of the common monster parts** used in the base recipe, rounded down.
- **0% of gems**.
- **0% of rare monster parts**.

An equipped item must be unequipped before it can be salvaged.

### Future recovery research

A research node/upgrade should later unlock partial recovery of precious enhancements:

| Recovery research | Gems returned | Rare monster parts returned |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 / unresearched | 0% | 0% |
| Level 1 | 20% | 20% |
| Level 2 | 35% | 35% |
| Level 3 | 50% | 50% |

All salvage calculations round down. Precious enhancement recovery should never reach 100%; crafting choices should retain some permanence and cost.

## 12. Persistence and run interaction

The following are **account/persistent progression**, not run-local:

- Gold
- Metal bars
- Gems
- Common monster parts
- Rare monster parts
- Empty Vials and crafted consumables
- Crafted equipment inventory
- Equipped equipment

The following remain run-local unless separately changed elsewhere:

- Current HP
- Keys
- Temporary floor pickups
- Run position/state

On a new run, player combat stats are rebuilt from:

1. baseline character stats,
2. permanent progression/upgrades,
3. level bonuses,
4. equipped crafted gear,
5. any temporary provision/run modifiers.

The current automatic `gear(quality)` starter-equipment model should ultimately be replaced by the persistent equipped-item loadout defined here.

## 13. Implementation constraints / acceptance criteria

When this design is implemented, the feature is complete only if all of the following are true:

- [ ] Generated treasure no longer raises gear quality directly.
- [ ] Every generated treasure chest grants Gold.
- [ ] Chests can drop progression-appropriate bars, gems, and Empty Vials using the tables above.
- [ ] No gem or metal can appear before its unlock floor/depth.
- [ ] Tower floor 70 / Delve depth 700 gives Amethyst a 0.10% base chest chance; floor 72 / depth 720 gives 0.30%.
- [ ] All four current enemies can drop their unique common and rare materials at the documented base rates.
- [ ] Enemy materials and chest materials persist across death and new runs.
- [ ] Gear has nine equip slots: weapon, shield, helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots, gloves, necklace, and ring.
- [ ] The Gear page contains Equipped, Inventory, and Crafting views.
- [ ] All six metal tiers can craft all nine equipment slots.
- [ ] Crafting consumes the documented bars/common parts and any optional gems/rare parts.
- [ ] Crafted items retain their individual enhancement rolls/material choices.
- [ ] Salvaging returns only bars/common parts until recovery research is unlocked.
- [ ] Salvaging cannot duplicate resources through rounding, undo, reload, or repeated actions.
- [ ] Clear-reward chests remain separate from generated treasure chests.

## 14. Balance notes for later playtesting

The exact numeric rates in this document are an initial balance target, not a claim that they are final. The most important invariants are:

- basic equipment should be craftable primarily from bars + common enemy parts;
- gems and rare enemy parts should feel meaningfully scarce;
- new precious materials should first appear at low probability and become more obtainable with progression;
- high-tier gear should be materially better without making lower-tier content instantly irrelevant;
- salvaging should reduce the pain of replacing gear without making crafting choices free to reverse;
- generated treasure should support the crafting economy rather than bypass it by awarding finished power directly.
